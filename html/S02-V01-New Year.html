1. New Year's Day

The two fighters clashed bronze swords.

Cronos and Rhea sat together in the Megara watching the games. Only one of these contestants could be the ultimate victor, and that came down to Rhea's choice. Of course, in this, as in so many things, she considered, and often deferred to, Cronos' advice. But at the end of the day, she was the Queen and he was simply the only man with whom she would have sex for the year. A year that was rapidly coming to an end.

"Note his use of the feet," Cronos whispered to her, leaning in close to experience as much of her as possible in his remaining time. He gestured to the fighter on their right. "He half-commits, then retreats before fully committing. He is very light on his feet. If he can make the combat last long enough, he will win."

"And the other does not?" Rhea asked.

"No," replied Cronos. "Watch; there! He hesitates, and then over-commits. If he does that at the wrong moment, he will open up his entire body." The men exchanged two more blows and then, as if directed by Cronos' critique, the man to their left exposed his ribs and a gash rapidly opened up across them. Unlikely to be fatal in the games, where it could be quickly treated, it was a resounding victory to the other. As one was dragged away, the other came to Rhea to be rewarded with a laurel crown - the symbol of all the winners in this event.

The herald sounded the trumpet and called out, "Let the candidates for the foot race present themselves." Eight men, including the clearly winded victor of the combat which had just taken place moved forward to the starting line on the right of the Megara. These eight had already won their individual rounds of single combat, and taken part in the wrestling event, where they had performed creditably.

As they formed up on the start line, Rhea watched intently. Winning was not, by itself, enough. The grace and balance of the runners counted for much, as did the ease with which they could cover the relatively short distance of the Megara. In real life, soldiers were required to run many furlongs without a break; a single furlong across the Megara should be accomplished in their stride.

Rhea signalled and the herald sounded the trumpet. As the note finished, all eight men sprinted across the floor until they crossed the finish line on their left.

"A fairly easy decision, I'd say," Cronos whispered to his wife, the Queen.

She smiled at him. As so often since he had become King, they saw things exactly the same way.

As the Sun went down, the stars started to emerge in the sky.

There would, of course, be no moon tonight, and the sisters of the Pleiades would only be showing their faces for a short while before following the Sun into his grave. That was how New Year was.

The men of the village had prepared the big bonfire during the day, and now the young girls brought a brand lit from the sacred flame to set fire to it. As they did so, it crackled and burst into life.

Rhea watched the sky turn and the sisters set in the west, following their brother the Sun. And now, the New Year underway, it was time for the ritual as old as time. She signalled to the priestesses and they followed her to the perimeter of the fire, and began the dance. Meanwhile, the wives stayed back, seated with their husbands.

Surrounding the fire, the men started to beat the drums, and women started to play the trumpets and flutes. Rhea felt her body begin to move to the timing of the music and tried to find that point of communion with the Goddess, mourning now for the loss of her brother/husband.

Around her, the other priestesses were doing the same thing: worshipping the Goddess with their supple movements at the same time as trying to reach the ecstatic state of communion.

"Waaaahhhh!" To her left she heard one of the priestesses reach the point of ecstasy, then point into the crowd at one of the men there. Called forward to worship, he left his wife and came to the priestess. Immediately, he joined in the dance opposite her, timing his movements to hers, a reflection of her purity and devotion to the Goddess.

"Wooooo..." And to her right, she heard another priestess reach the threshold and call a man forward. She redoubled her efforts to reach the threshold, dancing every faster, losing herself in the rhythm of the music. The wine she had drunk before the festival began was starting to take hold as well, leaving her ever more disconnected from the here and now and lost in the cycle of life and death with the Goddess.

As she slowly slipped from conscious thought through unconscious action into ecstasy, she became connected with all around her, and was aware of the increasing number of priestesses who had moved from dancing, through ecstasy, to sexual intercourse with their partners. All around her, the dance was becoming slower but more entwined, as the male and the female joined together in one human bond.

"Uwwww..." She slipped fully into the trance and as she did so, called her husband forward. This was his moment, in some sense, even more than it was hers. In the same way that the moment of the sunset belonged more to the Sun than it did to Her sister's eternal cycles.

"Come to me, my darling ..." she whispered, almost under her breath.

And then he was there, in front of her.

So big, so muscular, so strong. A perfect representation of the brother/husband, the ideal. And he moved with such grace, such suppleness. Somehow it almost seemed like a shame. But that was the nature of the cycle, and the Goddess had ordained it so at the beginning of time.

He danced opposite her, copying her movements: first consciously, then unconsciously. As he followed her into the unconscious ecstasy of the dance, he came closer and then reached out and put his hands on her. Now they were not just in step, but entwined. She continued the dance as he first pulled her towards him, then lifted her up. She put her arms around him and placed her head on his broad shoulder. He lowered her slightly, and then, still dancing, he entered her.

The fire burned. The music played. All around her, the other priestesses were doing exactly the same thing she was doing, all of them at different stages of completion. As they finished, they fell to the floor, replete, unmoving. In the circle around the dancers, the others watched on, some playing instruments, some clapping their hands and stamping their feet. The ritual was not over until all the priestesses had been satisfied.

Suddenly, she felt him spasming inside her as he ejaculated. As he let her go, she pulled away from him, back towards the fire and pointed an accusatory finger at him. "Who is this man?" she demanded.

From the surrounding circle, a man leapt forward to protect her, a bronze sword shining brightly in his hand. With a single thrust, he stabbed Cronos in the back, then pulled the sword back out. When Cronos had fallen to the ground, he slashed at his neck, and removed the head, handing it to Rhea. She held it by the hair, looking at it disdainfully, and then threw it into the fire.

"Cronos is dead! The old year is done!" cried the man who had killed Cronos, throwing away the sword.

Rhea moved closer to him and wrapped her arms around him. As he lifted her up and began pleasuring her, she shouted as loudly as she could manage, "Cronos is alive! Long live Cronos! The New Year is with us!"

The music kept playing.
