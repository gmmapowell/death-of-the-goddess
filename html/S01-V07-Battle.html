7. Battle

Shep rushed in from the west and into the Megara in front of Bougainvillaea.

She acknowledged his arrival and inclined her head to signal to him that he was welcome to speak. He drew in a few deep breaths, recovering from the efforts of running from the outer reaches of the settlement where he had been posted as a sentry today.

"From the southwest, a large cloud of dust. We think a group of warriors are coming to attack us."

Bougainvillaea considered this. It had certainly come to the time of year when groups could be tempted into attacks and raids, but the harvest had been excellent; usually such attacks came when the benefits of stealing from others outweighed the risks associated with it. If indeed this was such a raid, people would be dead by the end of the day. Mostly men, admittedly, possibly even just useless mouths in the winter ahead, but even so, Bougainvillaea had a maternalistic feeling for them.

On the other hand, mobilizing for an attack would disrupt all the activities of the village for the day; which, if the boy was in error, would be a terrible waste. At least it was not harvest time, but then nobody would be foolish enough to mount a raid instead of bringing in the harvest.

Shep might still be young, but she was confident that he had seen what he had seen and knew what it meant. A raiding party was almost undoubtedly coming up from the southwest and would be here before noon. If they were not prepared to meet it, the raiders would be able to enter the village unopposed and make off with anything they fancied - grain, tools, even the villagers. No, they must prepare and go out and meet the enemy beyond the outer reaches of the settlement.

"Shep, summon the leaders of the defenders. Go to the barracks of the men and summon all of them to the southwest boundary. Spread the word through the fields for all men to take their tools and gather at the southwest boundary."

Immediately, Shep ran off to raise the alert.

The Sun had risen nearly to full by the time that the majority of the defenders had gathered at the designated meeting point. It wasn't a point, as such, but an area in vaguely the right direction to meet the outsiders. When the first men or leaders appeared, they drifted towards each other, and that then became "the designated meeting place". As others arrived, they drifted to the same location where they could see the others already gathering. When the leaders arrived, they started to take stock of the situation and had directed various men to forward scouting positions and had tried to organise the rest of the men into some kind of formation so that they were more likely to scare off the enemy than injure each other.

Men were good at fighting. Sometimes Virbunum thought that it might be the only thing they were really good at. Often they did it amongst themselves for no apparent reason. But then, when you wanted them to fight, to protect the village, and tried to organise them in such a way that they actually defended the village, they would suddenly just start doing whatever they wanted, rather than focusing on the task in hand.

By now, the scouts were sending in frequent messages and it was clear that the enemy were coming up in a vague column pretty much directly at them. There appeared to be only four or five women leading them, so probably a smaller force than Viburnum had at her disposal (she had ten lieutenants to call on, and each of them should be able to corral three to four hands worth of men1). If they were well deployed, and managed to hold their formation and nerve, they should be able to hold their own against this lesser force. Considering all her options, she called her lieutenants to her and, drawing in the sand, outlined her array of battle.

She had decided to opt for the classic left-centre-right formation, and stack three units to each of the left and right to hold a "corridor". In the centre, she placed the other four units, further back towards the village in a square formation. She expected the oncoming attackers would come down and engage with the front two units and, once these were engaged, the two units behind could come around and join the fray. If her intelligence as to the enemy's numbers were correct, that would be about an even fight; when it reached its zenith, she would signal to the troops to left and right to leap up from their positions and crush the enemy. If all went well the enemy would recognize that they were outnumbered and run away. For form's sake, she would order her men to chase them for a few furlongs, but then let them escape. There was no merit in injuring more men - on either side - once the issue had been decided and the village protected.

Plans conveyed, she checked that all of the women understood what was expected of them, and they went and collected their relevant units of men and took them to the positions and everybody bent down, crouched down, lay down, hid or otherwise concealed themselves while the forward scouts continued to relay information about the oncoming units. When they were close enough - and still on the same course - Virbunum gave silent instructions that they should retreat back behind the defending soldiers. These men now formed the front line of the defence.

Soon, the singing cadence of the advancing enemy could be heard over the noise of their jogging footsteps. In weather like this, it never paid to run too fast, but a loping jog, combined with an appropriate song, could eat up miles with seemingly no effort - something that all the men (and the women who supervised them) had long since mastered. At a walking pace, it could take days to travel between large villages; at a jog, the job could be done (in both directions) in a single day.

Tamarisk had the front left unit of the central square. She was hiding in a gully near one of the cisterns. She, too, could hear the oncoming soldiers and could feel her heart beating faster. Soon, they would once again be in combat. Last time, one of her men had been killed and two others had been wounded. Hopefully, it would not be like that this time! She looked forward, expectantly, but could see nothing yet. On the other hand, the singing and stamping feet were getting closer every minute.

There they were! As if by magic, there was suddenly a line of men across her line of sight, stretching from off to her left to beyond her right. The men to her right would be Amaryllis' problem; those to her left would be initially unengaged, but she had to hope that the unit behind her would take them on before they had the opportunity to surround her unit, outnumbering and overwhelming them.

She watched carefully, now. The moment of engagement was the single most important decision that she had to make in the battle. Engage too soon and the element of surprise was lost; leave it too late and her men would still be trying to get into position while the other side would be sweeping at them with their scythes and prodding at them with the pruning-hooks. Not yet, not yet, but soon ...

"Ready, now!" she called quietly to the twenty men around her. "Be ready!"

The enemy kept coming. She couldn't tell if they knew she was there, in their path; if so, they were yet to give any sign by either pausing or charging.

"Now," she shouted. "Get them!"

Leaping up, the twenty men around her entered the fray, launching themselves towards the ten or so men opposite them in them line. Within seconds, scythes and pruning-hooks were flying and making contact with enemy weapons and bodies. Incredibly effective when used against plants, the weapons were much less effective against bodies, and many a crack would evince groans or shouts of pains, but would not obviously immobilise an opponent.

The other three central units were now up and involved in the melee, and the four commanders had fallen back behind the scrimmage, both to have a better view and to give directions as appropriate. As Virbunum had expected, the numbers now were roughly equal, although the enemy seemed to have a slight advantage, mainly grouped in the centre, but not enough to truly break through and outnumber any of their groups of men.

"Right, keep right!" Tamarisk shouted. As the pressure grew in the centre, the right-hand side of her unit was being forced to the left. If that carried on, the enemy would be able to push through and have spare men to come around the back. If that happened, the battle could be lost before Virbunum called in the reinforcements.

Suddenly, she heard the trumpet call. Three strong notes, one after another, called the wings into the battle. Now she knew it was just a dozen or two heartbeats before those extra warriors would be swinging into the battle. The scene in front of her intensified as the enemy tried to force their way through the middle between her unit and Amaryllis'. Then suddenly...

There was a crash as the outside units came sweeping in and across the line from behind the enemy. The almost doubling of home troops caused a sudden reversal in the pressure on the line and in a moment, everything became disorganised chaos. What had been almost a unit-against-unit, line-against-line battle suddenly became every man for himself, and there was the danger of the village's men attacking their friends. Tamarisk called to them to identify themselves.

"Mykenai! Mykenai!" they shouted; and every man from the village was simultaneously shouting and listening for the name. None of them attacked anyone else shouting the name of their village. In the uproar, the enemy turned towards their homes and fled.

"Chase them!" shouted Tamarisk; and Amaryllis; and the other commanders. "Chase them back where they came from!"

All the villagers set off, chasing the enemy, howling their battle slogans and waving their weapons. But the enemy were generally faster. Whenever they were being caught, they would throw away their weapons, and anything else they were carrying, just in order to go faster.

A few minutes later, Virbunum ordered the long blast sounded on the trumpet that indicated that the chase should come to an end. The men stopped, and Tamarisk and the other commanders were able to catch up to them.

"Mykenai! Tamarisk! To Me!" called Tamarisk, as the other commanders did the same. Slowly, panting for breath, all of her soldiers gathered about her. She quickly cast about, and identified all the members of her unit as still present, alive, and, so it would seem, largely uninjured. They continued to look out over the wilderness at the enemy as they continued to retreat.

"Good work, all of you," she congratulated them. "We will wait here for another finger of the Sun, but I do not think they will return; they know we were too good for them. Then we will return, collecting such things as they left behind. They will make good spoils to Bougainvillaea to remind us of this day."

As they stood there, the same dust cloud that had heralded the arrival of the enemy in the morning showed them shuffling off into the desert.

The enemy had not been so lucky as Tamarisk's unit. In total, they had left behind six of their number, four dead and two injured sufficiently badly that they were unable to retreat with the others. On their return to the village, the men tipped the dead into the cisterns as an offering to the Goddess who had no doubt been active in helping them this day. The two injured men, along with one of the village men who had suffered a broken leg, were taking back to the village, along with a handful of scythes and pruning-hooks that had been collected from the battlefield. All of the spoils were taken to the Megara and a trophy was built before Bougainvillaea was summoned.

"Praise the Goddess!" she exclaimed. "We have been very fortunate to be delivered from this enemy today." She turned to the captives: "Tell me, where are you from, and why did you attack us?"

"We are from Ogdoi-li, and we attacked because the Goddess sent a sign saying that she was displeased with you."

"And why is the Goddess displeased with us?"

"I am just a lowly man, I do not have the ear of the Goddess. I just know what I was told yesterday: that we would attack you because the Goddess in her displeasure had ordered it."

Bougainvillaea nodded sagely. Yes, probably that is all this man knew. If she had been going to find anything more out, she would have needed to capture one of the women in charge. But that would have been very tricky. But at least they had come through relatively unscathed, and had some fine new tools. She just needed to get them back in working order.

1 One hand being able to count up to five, so four hands would be twenty; thus the total force would be up to 200.
