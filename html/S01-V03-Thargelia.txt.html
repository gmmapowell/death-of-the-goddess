6 Thargelia

Pip was up before dawn.

In itself, this was not surprising, as he was given charge of the early morning watch. But today was special, and he knew it. When he had been woken up from his sleep in the middle of the night, the moon was just rising to the east. When the moon showed half her face like this, coming up at the dead center of the night, while her elder sister was still asleep, everybody knew the month was a quarter through. And this month - the first month of the entire year, the first month that the younger sisters were back awake in the morning sky - just over there, in fact, rising in the east, hurrying to be up before their big sister - would be the festival of the first fruits.

There was no question they were ready. The barley was standing in fields, waiting to be plucked. The fig trees were hanging with fruit, possibly not quite ripe but the Sisters never seemed to agree amongst themselves exactly when it was in the year, so who was he to say whether it was too early for the offerings? The almond trees would be ready - they always were. He'd been hoeing around the beans a little while back - before the season started, anyway - and the leaves there had looked good; although you never could tell with beans.

To the east, he saw the sky beginning to brighten; as it did, he watched the stars slowly retreat from the sky, just as they did every morning. Hiding their faces in worship as Sister Eldest took to the stage. Only Second Sister sometimes had the temerity to hold on to her place in the sky during the day when Sister Eldest was out and about. And only sometimes.

Sprig was asleep at his feet; as a messenger, she wasn't needed until there was a message to run. Until now, as it happened tonight, so she was free to sleep until he needed her. Gently, he nudged her with his foot. As a mere girl, she was his social equal, and this was acceptable. He would obviously have shown proper reverence to a Lady asleep at his feet. Sprig came awake instantly and silently. To do otherwise would quite possibly endanger everyone in the village - depending on why she was being woken of course.

"Run to Bougainvillaea and tell her that She is arising from Her slumbers. And that her sister has crossed the midline this morning. Today is the appointed day. Bougainvillaea must organise us for the Thargelia." Immediately on her feet, Sprig started running for Bougainvillaea's hut.

Bougainvllaea was awake, of course. Sprig had expected as much when she had been sent: whenever she had been sent to alert her in the past, she had always been awake. Now, as then, she slowed from her run before she reached the porch of the leader's hut and approached slowly, eyes downcast. When she arrived, she bowed meekly from the waist and waited to be addressed.

"Sprig, isn't it?" Bougainvillaea asked at last. "No doubt Pip has sent you to alert me to the Thargelia. I'm sure he knew I would know, but it is good of him to stick to protocol nevertheless. Go in and notify the other Ladies. They will wish to collect themselves before She fully rises this morning. Turning to her two assistants, she said, "Come, let us go and prepare ourselves to greet Her as she rises on this most special morning." Slowly, she walked down the steps from the porch and led the way to the Megara.

Forgotten, ignored, Sprig spent a few moments looking after them, wishing that she was like them; hoping that, one day, she might be like them. Then she turned and went to the other huts. Everybody in the village had to be roused for this day of all days; but she would start with the leading Ladies, for they should be first is acknowledging Her, and then in giving the gifts that had come from Her in that endless cycle that was life-in-death.

Bougainvillaea stood with arms raised as the Sun's face breached the horizon. Today, the sky was clear and the Sun shone Her face down on all the villagers as they gathered in the Megara. A good sign, Bougainvillaea told herself, that She would choose to show herself in all her glory on this day of praise. She started to intone the ritual prayer of greeting:

               

We greet you, O Sun,        

Visible face of Her we adore,        

Giving to us all things        

And asking us for nothing        

As we receive, so we shall share        

With those of your persons        

With whom we share this life                        

Today is the day of collection        

In which we gather our bounty        

We share with all who live        

And hold nothing back.        

Today is the first day of the season        

Today is the glimmering of life        

For which we give your praise.

When she had finished, she held her hands out to the sun, and then began again, all the Ladies behind her joining her in worshipping the Sun as it cleared the horizon. For moments more they stood there, entranced by the glory of what was happening, and what was about to happen.

When the Sun was about two fingers' width above the horizon, Bougainvillaea clapped her hands together and led the way down the steps from the Megara and out into the fields. She walked through the barley and, when she found some particularly ripe and delicious-looking ears, she plucked them. Turning to Chrysanthemum, she placed them in the platter she had made especially for this day. The holy platter. The platter of offering. The platter itself was part of the offering - the first thing Chrysanthemum had made since the turning of the seasons. And the beauty of its decoration, and the arrangement of the fruits on it, was all part of the offering. The offering must be perfect, for in what other way could these mere villagers express their undying gratitude for all that they would receive this year? Could they really consider themselves worthy of all this plenty if they did not do their utmost to prepare the ultimate offering for the Goddess?

In the orchard, she reached up and picked the ripest fruits. Like Pip, she could never understand why all the different manifestations of the Goddess seemed to be ready at different times. But the Sisters in the Sky could not be denied - everybody knew that this was the time to make the offering and if the sprites in the fig trees were a little behind this year - and they were - then she would just pluck the best, juiciest and ripest figs to share. Bougainvillaea was confident She would understand. She, in her infinite wisdom, would accept that the villagers had tried, had not stinted in their efforts, or held back in any way. She would know that Bougainvillaea had walked through the orchard every day this year, observing the growth of the fruit and what would be ready.

From the orchard she led the procession to the field of beans. Each year, one field was sown with beans while the others held the essential barley. The Goddess had ordained it so, and had punished villages in the past who had forsaken this rule. Neither barley nor beans grew when the rule was broken: a clear punishment for wayward children. The beans were the most difficult fruit to select: being underground, you could only go on the health of the leaves on the surface. She had enlisted the help of Tamarisk over the past few days to select the seemingly healthiest specimens. Tamarisk was an expert when it came to beans.

She signalled Tamarisk forward now. In her hand, Tamarisk held the sacred shovel, the special shovel which was obligatory on these holy occasions. Tensing slightly, for the occasion was important beyond words, Tamarisk placed the shovel at the root of the bush Bougainvillaea indicated - the one that they had identified a few days before.

She drove the shovel into the soil in a single, fluid motion. The soil broke easily and she pulled it back out. Stepping to her right, she drove it down again. Again, and again she made the ritual movements until the plant had been broken away from the soil on all the cardinal points. Now Bougainvillaea herself came forward and communed with the plant. "As I was pulled from my mother, so I pull you from your mother, the earth." With a swift tug, she pulled on the stem of the plant and it came away, soil draining from it as she lifted it into the air.

As she brushed the excess soil away, she could see that Tamarisk had, indeed, chosen brilliantly: these beans were shining brilliantly in the early morning sun. She continued to brush away the excess soil, then called Poinsettia forward with a ewer filled with water. Holding the beans away from her, but with the stem over the hole from which they came, she intoned, "as we take from you, Our Mother, so we return unto you that which we will not eat," and, with that, Poinsettia carefully washed the beans and the mixture of soil and water fell back into the hole, leaving behind a clean bean bush.

Magnolia stepped forward, bearing a new, clean towel she had finished weaving on the eve of New Year's Day. She held the towel under the bean bush and started to dry the beans. As she did so, water and soil ran off the beans and discoloured the fabric. When she was done, Bougainvillaea held a gleaming bush. She passed it to Tamarisk, and then, carefully, gently, started removing the beans from the bottom and placing them on the platter in an arrangement she had been thinking about for days - without knowing exactly how many beans she would have to make the arrangement.

From here, they walked out to the west. Here were the almond trees, uncontrolled but growing here for centuries. Truly a gift of the Goddess. As the procession reached the trees, they stopped and Bougainvillaea reached up and selected the first ripe nuts of the season to add to the platter. Her arrangement complete, she turned to the people and said, "All have seen us gather the fruits to offer to the Goddess. Our offering is collected; it is the first of the fruits of this season; we are proud of our offering. Now, those who are chosen shall continue; the lesser shall return to the village to spend the day in fasting and meditation that the Goddess may look down at us and see the respect we have for her."

With this, most of the Ladies and all of the men and children turned and went back to the village. When they were gone, Bougainvillaea looked at the four Ladies who remained with her. The chosen. The leaders. Between the five of them were held all the secrets of life: of barley, of trees, of beans, of pottery, of weaving. None were closer to the Goddess. In turn, they passed the secrets on to their daughters and to others in the community. These were not intentional secrets, but rather, it took years to assimilate the care required in each of the crafts.

With the others gone, they carried on westwards, out towards where they knew the Sun would set and hide Her face from them. Where everything went at the end of its time: the Sun, her sister the Moon and her lesser sisters all made the same transit, day by day, from their rising in the East to their setting in the West. In the same way, Bougainvillaea knew, the souls of man travelled from the origins in the East Wind to their setting out here, in the cisterns of the western wilderness.

They walked carefully, in the prescribed formation, with the platter - and Chrysanthemum - protected in the middle of a diamond with the other four taking the cardinal points. There was no hurry to this celebration; the Goddess knew that a whole year had gone into the preparation for this moment: after all, had she not done most of the work? In her form as the Sun, she had shone on the crops; as the Clouds, she had sent rain; as the Rivers she had sent water when there was no rain. Everywhere they turned, the Goddess was there, helping them, providing them with what they needed. So no, there was no hurry. Just respect. Respect for the Goddess and for all her manifestations.

They reached the designated spot, in amongst the cisterns. Chrysanthemum placed the platter on the ground, bowed low before it, and the five women retreated twenty paces back to the east to watch and see how their offering would be received by the Goddess. Favourably, they all hoped. Slowly, from behind them and to their left, the Goddess' face climbed higher into the sky.

It was mid-afternoon by the time the first snake came up out of the cisterns to look around. This was a propitious sign: neither so quickly that the Goddess was angry at their tardiness in offering to her, nor taking so long as to show disdain for their offering. She crawled slowly across the dry, broken ground and then went once around the platter before lifting her head to look at what she had been offered.

As she moved her head, a gentle sibilant hissing could be heard. Slowly, she reached forward and opened her mouth, probing a fig with her tongue. Then, hesitancy dismissed, she jerked her head forward rapidly and swallowed the fig in a single motion. They could almost see it as it flowed along her body. Again, the gentle hiss, a hiss of pleasure as she continued to examine the platter in front of her.

As they watched, others came slithering up from the cisterns and approached the platter. Once one had accepted the offering, others followed suit. Within minutes, a dozen or more snakes were slithering over the platter, consuming the offering until it was all gone. The hissing of the snakes expressed pleasure, not disappointment, and Bougainvillaea was pleased that her offering had been accepted by the Goddess' representatives on earth.

This would be a good year. She was sure of it.
