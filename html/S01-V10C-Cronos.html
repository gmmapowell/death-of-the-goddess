11. Cronos

Shep ran in from the northern edge of the village, near the forests.

"An army comes ... from the north," he gasped as he reached the Megara and was acknowledged by Bougainvillaea and the other women there.

"How big?" asked Bougainvillaea, confused both by the message and the direction. No villages were to be found to the north; just the forest, and, beyond that, the mountain ridges. Beyond the mountains, they said, there was water.

Like all the other villagers, Shep could count to five using the fingers of his right hand, and sometimes up to ten if he used both hands. Armies were counted by the number of women leaders they brought, sometimes with banners.

But this army was not like that.

"It is different," he said, and attempted to qualify. Probably they needed to send one of the women who was experienced in such things. He was just a runner really - able to take the message from the borders of the village to the leaders quickly. "There are more than five men coming. But they have no leaders. It seems to be just men. And they look different. They are not men like our men."

"They have no women?" asked Bougainvillaea, simply failing to comprehend what she was being told.

"We think they have some women with them, and some animals. But they are at the back. The men seem to be deciding things."

Bougainvillaea and Virbunum exchanged glances, confused. But Virbunum knew that dealing with this was her responsibility and she quickly took charge.

"Summon the army and tell them to meet at the northern edge of our lands, where the trail to the forest begins. When they are assembled, they will move forward to the observation point. I will meet them there." And Shep ran off to do as he was told.

Virbunum turned to Bougainvillaea and confided, "the signs this year have confused us. There is a possibility that this is something we have never seen before. Men taking the lead from women would be ... unprecedented."

Bougainvillaea nodded sagely, "I cannot understand how they hope to succeed without women to direct them; and yet, surely they would not appear as an army if they were just a band of surplus men from some other village that could not afford to support so many useless mouths through the winter.

"And this talk of animals confuses me too. Animals are following the men? Or have they taken them captive somehow? How is that possible? Do they have some enchanters with them who can control the spirits of men and of animals and enchant them?"

"There is only one way to find out more and that is to go and observe them. I will not have long. It would be better if we had a full cycle of the moon to observe them, but we do not. It is possible that having come this far, they will not come to our village but go elsewhere but ... I am not sanguine. I feel that this event has been foretold in the stars."

And she turned and left.

Was this it? Or was this just an advance party?

Shep, for all his inadequacy in describing this - she searched for a word - group, he had not been wrong in any particular. It was certainly small - she estimated about four hands of men, total - and, as Shep had said, there were no women leading it. At the back, again, as Shep had said, there was a party of women and some kind of animals - maybe the goats that you could find in the mountains - tagging along behind. But these women were clearly not in charge of this party. That would be that man there.

Bigger than most men she had seen, barrel-chested, and with a flame-red beard. He had a loincloth around his waist, but other than that, he was naked. Around his loincloth, he had some kind of ornamented belt, and hanging from that he had some kind of weapon - bronze, certainly - about the size of a club, but thinner. She was not sure what it was, but it was presumably the weapon of the leader.

Except, as she looked more closely, all of the men in this party had the same arrangement: lioncloth, ornamented belt, and bronze club. Yes, she had to presume that these were weapons and that, somehow, some group of men had developed the intelligence to move from place to place - and presumably fight - on their own.

She was tempted to just try a frontal battle. The numbers were so in her favour that she didn't even understand how they would be able to stand against her numbers; probably they would run away in the face of determined resistance.

But caution tugged at her. There was so much here she didn't understand; so much that could be hidden. Was this, in fact, just a feint, a decoy, intended to lull them into false confidence? And then bring up the reserve party to batter them when they were fully engaged? Could that even be done?

Nothing in her experience had fully prepared her for this moment.

She decided to go with the tried and tested. Four units would form the centre and should easily outnumber this rabble. Then she had ten units left and could send five to each side. She certainly had the numbers but couldn't help feeling that what she was missing was much more important than that.

The enemy kept on coming, apparently oblivious to the presence of an army in front of them. They were just ambling, not obviously ready to do battle or scouting ahead for any signs of danger.

As Tamarisk gave the signal, Polo jumped up and charged towards the oncoming group. He brandished his bronze-edged scythe at the man opposite who was yet to draw his weapon. As he swung, the man simply ... disappeared. Polo, committed to the swing, overbalanced and had to adjust his feet to regain balance. And there was the man again, with his bronze club in his hand, ready to swing, in front of him.

Except he didn't swing the club.

Rather, more like he would wield a pruning-hook, the man thrust the club into his chest, where it went - straight through. Polo looked down at himself in wonder, as blood poured out of the wound. A few heartbeats later, he collapsed to the ground and the man opposite him used his foot to contemptuously push Polo to the ground, dead.

Tamarisk saw the same thing happen across the line as they advanced. What should have been a melee, leading to the enemy being surrounded and disarmed due to their limited numbers, was becoming a massacre of the men under her command. Even as the second wave went in, she could see that they held no advantage in terms of surprise, tactics or numbers. Whatever the weapons were that the enemy wielded, they must carry some magic from the Goddess. They killed on contact, and the men carrying them were simply more adept, more adroit, at moving in combat. Even outnumbered, they were simply winning. In all her years, Tamarisk had never seen this many men literally killed in a field of battle. It was just too hard to kill a man.

As she watched, another hand of men fell to the ground. She wrung her own hands, indecisive. Should she send in the reinforcements, hoping that being surrounded would make the difference? Or should she concede now, while she had the strongest negotiating position? After all, if the enemy clearly demonstrated that they could win a comprehensive victory, she would have nothing to negotiate with.

She made the fateful decision.

She indicated to the trumpeter to play the sounds for "reform" that would bring the wings back to the rear, where they could offer a defence in depth. And then she sounded the "disengage" that would cause the men engaged in fighting to pull pack. Not that there really were any men actively engaged in fighting; there were those who were dying, and those who had not yet attempted to engage the enemy.

She waited while all the forces at her disposal formed up behind her in their units with the women in command ahead of them. When all was in order, she took the ritual steps forward and bowed to her opponent - or, at least, the man she was assuming was here leading this unnatural force.

She spread her hands wide to include all the forces behind her and indicate that what she said was said to include them all: this was not merely her surrender but the surrender of the whole village. Assuming, of course, a peaceful resolution could be had.

She fixed her expression into the neutral shape needed to frame the negotiations of a lost village. She had done this before, but never after such an uncompromising loss. She had never lost this many men before in either a loss or a victory. But now she must acknowledge the loss and extract what she could from it.

She rose up again, arms still stretched wide, and spoke the formal words of submission.

"We accept that you are the stronger party," she said.

She waited, expecting him to acknowledge in turn that he would be magnanimous in victory, but he just stood there, eyeing her with a cruel expression in his eyes: did he want to keep on killing? What would be the benefit of that?

She tried again.

"We accept that you are the stronger party."

Again, no response. She was tempted to give orders to her lieutenants to attack again. If this man wanted to fight, then they could fight. If he wasn't going to accept her surrender and negotiate, it was unclear what he would accept. And if he was so obstreperous as to not accept anything, they would end up fighting: that was inevitable. But surely he could see that in that case, both sides would lose.

She tried to pretend that he had acknowledged her submission and moved on to the next line in the formula.

"We offer you to come and join us in our feast. Let us sit, and share barley with each other and with the Goddess, and let us discuss our differences in the world. And then, full and replete with the necessities of life, let us exchange gifts and part as friends."

Exchanging gifts being code for "what is the least you will accept to go away and leave us alone", thought Tamarisk, not for the first time. Although she could not figure what it would be like to be friends with these stony-faced murderers, she thought. They do seem to really relish the violence. Will they parley? Or will they attack, even against superior numbers?

He stood there, looking at her, intently.

She had no idea what to do.

Through the red mist, Cronos realised he was unopposed.

He stood back, shaking slightly with the battle rage he always felt at times like this. He had killed three or four men already, but now the others had backed off. He shook his head slightly. Were they admitting defeat? Were they regrouping? He and his men were still massively outnumbered. Should he retreat while given the opportunity? Was it a trick?

None of it made sense.

As he watched and wondered, more and more men - with occasional women dotted throughout the force - came from the sides and from behind him to join the ranks of those opposing him. Surely if all of these had joined the fight, they would have won? Man for man, he acknowledged he was superior, but ultimately the numbers would have told. Particularly if they had come at him from behind.

One woman stepped forward from the line. Was she an offering? Were they accepting that they could have won, but would rather not take the losses and would rather bribe him with women and goats? If so, where were the rest? Or was this a token?

She bowed down before him and stretched her arms wide. When she rose up again, she had a strange expression. Not violent, but not submissive either. Whatever she was, she was not an offering.

"Ma-hey-ga-li ga-li-po-da ga-ne-ne," she said, or something like that.

He signalled his men to follow.

Whatever this woman was doing, she clearly didn't want to fight any more. But was she leading him into a trap? But what trap could they have laid that was a better position that the one in which they had started?

They were certainly retreating in disciplined formation. He considered attacking again as they followed, but, at the moment, what was the point? They'd certainly had the better of the earlier fight, but in war there were never any guarantees, and presumably they would finally let him meet the leader of their tribe, their King, whatever, and he would be able to negotiate the right to share this land with these people. After all, it seemed the Gods had been very clear they wanted him to come here. Why would that be if they let him down at the last step?

After a while, he could set yet more people ahead. Most of these were women, which made sense since the fighting force had been mainly men. This would presumably be the campsite where they had left the women when they went off to fight, although he didn't see anywhere for people to live. As they came closer, he could see that there were bowls on the ground, and people arranging all kinds of expensive pottery. Even his father did not have this many pots! For sure, these people were rich - and yet did not have the strength to defend their wealth against any intruder. How, again, was that possible?

The men dispersed away, around the back of the women and the food. The women who had been with them turned and faced him, and again, the woman who he presumed must be standing in for the leader bowed to him and waved her arms wide and muttered something he still had no hope of understanding.

But a feast he understood. He stepped forward and said, "you welcome us to a guest-feast. I see the bowls and finery you have laid out. We will share of our plenty." And, signalling to one of the boys at the back of the column, he said, "we offer a goat in the name of the great God." The boy brought forward a goat and placed the lead in his hand.

Quickly, women came forward and prepared the fire pit and the spit. They opened the holy jar and pushed in a stick, which caught alight. They lit the fire and then, with great ceremony, he slit the throat of the goat, allowing the blood to fall on the ground.

"With this blood, I sanctify this meeting place. Here we share in guest rights and acknowledge that we work together in good faith to good ends. Then let the duel decide for all."

When the goat had bled out, the women quickly dressed it and mounted it on the spit.

"Dionysus, the wine!"

They had little enough wine left; unlike water, which ran in streams, the wine was made somewhere and then traded throughout the mountains. More than once in the past couple of years of travelling, he had encountered other parties who had some to trade, or occasionally, to surrender in the face of death. But many more times, they had drunk the wine that they had, and ever its quantity went down. But, limited as their supplies were, this was definitely an occasion to celebrate.

Another woman had come to join the first. They had talked to each other for a long while, but neither of them had tried talking to him since they had settled down here. Was this one the wife of the chief? Why were all the men hanging back and letting these women do the talking for them? Where was the chief of this tribe?

Oh well, probably everything would become clear as the feast went on. Maybe the men did not want the shame of sharing the guest feast when they had lost in battle.

The meat had roasted nicely, their hosts were waiting patiently with their bowls of fruits, and now Cronos offered the wine. The woman who seemed to be in charge accepted a little and drank it, and tried to keep her face straight, but clearly did not like it. But the gesture was enough. If she didn't want it, that meant more for him and his men. He would, of course, pour out a cup for each of the leaders. Anything less would be an insult. But he expected to have a good night. Even before the ritual offerings began.

One of the women came over and gave him a bowl. It had some kind of seeds in it; he hadn't see anything quite like that before, but they seemed to have no end of them. He signalled one of the boys, who took some of the meat from the goat over to the woman leader. She acknowledged his gift and ate it.

Whatever the seeds were that they had given him, they were certainly filling and nutritious. They did a good job of soaking up the grease from the goat. This was a good combination. He should get one of his women to find out what the plant looked like from which they collected them.

He took another drink of wine and watched the women. They were enjoying their seeds, and partaking of the goat in suitable quantities. But none of them showed any interest in the wine.

"Dionysus," he called. "Do you think these people have never had wine? They seem to drink enough water, but no wine."

"Probably not," replied Dionysus. "I once spent a season in a valley where they made it from small fruits, although I don't know how. And I've met a lot of people who've never heard of it. Sad for them," he added, smiling lopsidely.

Bougainvillaea felt her head hurting.

Leaving aside that they had no words in common, nothing he said or did seemed to fit with any experience she had had of negotiation. For starters, she had never negotiated with a man before: the very thought that one had the temerity - not to mention ability - to do so confused her. It clearly wasn't that the women of their tribe had all died: they were here, and relegated to the back somewhere along with these strange animals they had brought with them, one of which they had just slaughtered as if they had hunted it.

And she could not read him. Maybe that was just because he was a man, but, as the feast had gone on, he had become louder and more belligerent. But now the feast was over. And presumably this was the time to negotiate. She stood up in order to address him:

"We do not know from where you come or how the Goddess brought you here, but we would see you peaceably settled and not at war with you. But how do we communicate?"

He stood up too and garbled something she did not understand. Then he waved his arm around in the air before pointing at her and garbled some more. Then, when she did not respond, he pulled his loincloth to one side, exposing his partially erect penis and pointed to it. Then started stroking it.

She was baffled. Did he mean to ejaculate on the floor in the middle of where they had had the holy feast? Or was he asking her if she would like to have sex with him? Neither seemed at all likely, but then nothing he said or did seemed to make any sense. He jabbered again. She stared at him blankly, trying to make any of it make any sense at all.

Suddenly, without warning, he leapt forward, grabbed her right wrist and turned her, thrusting her face down on her knees towards the floor. Before she could grasp what was happening, he had removed her loincloth and cast it to one side. A weight like a boulder crashed onto her back, forcing her to the ground. And then, with a violent tearing, she felt his penis thrust inside her. "Owww..."

What the hell was happening?

The feast was over, and Cronos was pleasantly drunk.

None of these women had drunk any of their wine: they had been happy enough to have their share of his goat, though, so they weren't completely distrusting.

Now, he just needed to figure out where the leader of this tribe was hiding and get him to come out and challenge him to a duel. Once he was dead, he could have this woman and everybody would agree that he was now the ruler of this tribe and, in lieu of goats, he would accept the village with these strange fruits as his dowry.

The woman he presumed must be the wife of the chief stood up and harangued him. Her words hadn't made any sense when he was sober, and he didn't think that would change now. His pleasant drunk was starting to morph into an angry, frustrated drunk and the last thing he wanted right now was to be lectured by some woman. He stood up too.

"Look, woman, I don't know where your husband is, the chief of your tribe, whatever. But if he doesn't come out right now and battle me for the rights to you and this land, I'm just going to take it for myself. You can't stop me, can you?"

She looked at him, blankly. As if that would make him change him mind.

"Your husband, woman! The one who sticks this in you." And he pulled aside his loincloth, revealing his penis, already becoming erect at the thought of both killing her husband and then having her here, in the middle of everyone, demonstrating his prowess in the face of the people, her dead husband and all the Gods.

She still didn't get it. Uppity bitch. Finally, he lost patience.

"Well, if he's just going to hide away, he loses his rights. Come here, and I'll fuck you anyway, and then I will be your master if he can't be bothered to fight for you."

He gave her a moment to come to her senses, but she did not: she just stood there, staring at him like some kind of halfwit. In a well-practised battle move, he stepped forward, pulled on her wrist and twisted her to the ground. Whipping off her loincloth, he knelt down behind her and entered her.

Nobody tried to stop him.
