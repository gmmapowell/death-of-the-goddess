B. Author's Note

There are quite a few notes at the end of this book. But my book, my rules.

First off, this book is a lot longer - or possibly just much broader in scope - than I had originally intended. The book took life from Robert Graves' assertion in King Jesus that the Roman Kings followed a practice of matrilineal inheritance and that, despite the obfuscations he had tried to put in, it was clear to anybody who read Livy what was going on. I read Livy again, and decided that, for all the obfuscations, he must be right. And I thought it would be cool to go back and "unpack" that, and try and tell the story in a way that removed those obfuscations.

That was twenty years ago.

As I did research, now and again, I came up with more and more evidence that not only was this true, but it was true across Europe - and probably across the world - for one very simple reason: until very, very recently, every woman knew who her children were, but not a single man could actually be sure. And, when you think about wealth and inheritance, you realize how very important it is to know who your children are if you want them to inherit from you (which appears to be a basic animal instinct).

And the longer this went on, the more I realized that telling the story I wanted to tell - basically what amounts to Volume 4 here - depended on so much history that I needed to provide at least an extensive prologue. Which itself needed a prologue. You get the picture.

As I reached the end of planning and starting writing, I wondered if I actually needed another volume, describing the Trojan War. You see, as you look at it, there is no reason not to believe that we are talking about a war between male-dominated kingdoms in Greece and a still matrilineal society in Troy. Maybe, maybe not. It's very tempting to say again that Homer glossed over that, but given that all our accounts of the Trojan War only took written form hundreds of years after the event - and were composed by the victors - it wouldn't surprise me if all those details went missing in the interim. I may still go back and write that; the biggest obstacle is that it is a legend that has been covered so well and so often that it almost feels impossible to "retell" it now - especially when the devil is all in the details.

Of course, having decided to do that, the fourth volume here is a lot shorter than it would otherwise have been, and lots of detail has been left out. If your favourite Livy story didn't make it, I'm sorry. Hopefully the world of Hyancinth and Sprig made up for it.
