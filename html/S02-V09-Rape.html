Cronos yelled.

"That is no way to use a sword, even a wooden one." Carrin backed away and looked at Cronos resentfully. As only a part time soldier, he always felt inadequate in these drills, and frequently unfairly criticized. How was he supposed to be as good as the full-timers, who spent all day, every day practising?

"You need to keep the blade under control at all times," Cronos clarified at a more reasonable volume. "If you don't have a strong grip, then your opponent will just bash it out of your hand. Come here!"

Carrin walked over to him, his sword held in what he rather hoped was the "ready" position. "Let's go; hit me!" yelled Cronos. Carrin raised his sword and swung with all his strength. The next thing he knew, he was on the floor. Cronos kicked him and he rolled over. Cronos put his foot on his chest and smashed the wooden sword out of his hand.

"What use are you to the defence of the village like that?" he demanded and walked away.

Unsure what was expected of him, Carrin stood up and went over to collect his sword. Picking it up, he walked back to where the other men were still drilling and took his place in the line of "defenders". Within a few strokes, he had been disarmed again, his wooden sword flying through the air. As he turned to collect it, he saw Cronos grimacing at him again.

This was going to be a long day.

When he was dismissed with the other part-timers around noon, he walked down to the river to wash his sweat off. He was frustrated, angry and feeling hard done to. He had never asked to be part of the "army" and knew he wasn't any good at it. Why couldn't he be left in the fields, hoeing and carrying things for the others? Why this insistence that surely every man wanted to whack other men with a sword?

When he reached the river, he was alone. Most of the men had gone straight back to the village; meanwhile, he had avoided the company of the other two men who had come down to the river, deliberately walking towards the bend where some of the women were often to be found doing the laundry in the river. He thought he might be able to work some of his frustrations out by helping them.

When he arrived, Amphissa was there, trying to beat a chlamys dry with a couple of rocks.

"Hey," he said as he walked up towards her. "How are you getting on with that laundry in the hot sun?"

"Well," she replied, smiling up at him. "The heat certainly makes the clothes dry more quickly, but it's making me sweat into the one I'm wearing."

"You could always take it off and wash it now," he joked to her, smiling.

"I suppose I could, but probably best not," she said. "I feel like I'm almost there; a few more fingers of the sun and I should be finished."

He looked at where she was up to and offered to help: "I may not have as much skill as you, and I may not be able to use a sword, but I should be able to beat that with a rock for a few fingers." That, at least raised a smile and a small giggle.

"My husband was good with a sword, until he got himself killed," she confided. "I don't know if you knew him?"

"Macareus, right? I can't say I knew him well; like you say, he was good with a sword and was in the army full-time. I do know he used to beat me bloody when we did the army drills. But then, that doesn't tell you much. Everyone beats me bloody. I hate it." As he spoke, he continued to pound the clothes with the smooth rocks, the intensity of the pounding growing as he let his rage and frustration boil over. Why did everyone seem to think that being able to wave a sword around was so important in life? It really wasn't. And even if you were good at it, you could end up dead, leaving a gorgeous woman like this behind.

"Did you have any children?" he asked, knowing that in reality he should know this. He didn't know either of them well, but it wasn't like the village was huge. But, in reality, he was just making conversation while he worked. Why did he care if they had had children? It was unlikely he would ever have any himself; what woman would be foolish enough to tie her future prospects to his dismal standing in the community? And what did he need children for? He would never amass enough wealth in this life to pass anything on. He played with the village children from time to time; that was enough - he didn't need his own.

"No," she responded, a little downcast. She still hadn't decided whether she felt freer back belonging to the Goddess, particularly after the ritual of the Thesmophoria, or whether she would rather have wandered the village with the ultimate status symbol - a baby girl - tied to her back. It would have been hard to have managed on her own, but by the time it had become a toddler it could have played with the other children while she did her jobs around the village.

Carrin put down the rock and held up the garment he had been pounding while they had talked. Most of the water had been beaten out of it, and it was lying flat, all the puffiness ground out. He passed it to Amphissa, who tested it and nodded approval.

"It will dry completely if we leave it in the sun for a few fingers," she said, smiling at him. "Thank you for your help. You may not be any good with a sword, but you are good at pounding rocks."

Something snapped in Carrin, even before he realised it. "I may not be good with a sword in my hand, but there is nothing wrong with my own sword!" he rasped, pulling aside his loincloth to reveal his large erection.

She looked at him, frozen like a boar in the hunt. What was he trying to say? Was this some kind of competition where he was asking her to judge between him and her dead husband? It wasn't even like she hadn't seen dozens of erect penises over the years at the frequent festivals. Even her father's was probably better than his, though. She tried to be conciliatory.

"I'm sure there isn't," she began, "but I'm not sure that it's all that important." She looked at him dubiously, and that was all it took to push him over the edge. Leaping towards her, he pushed her backwards, and mounted her. Holding her down with his left hand, he whipped her loincloth away with his right and entered her with force and violence. She screamed but could do nothing to free herself from his huge weight. For what seemed like an eternity, he thrust inside her until she felt his wetness come inside her. Cronos pointed to the runner on duty, a girl of twelve summers.

"Fetch Carrin. I don't care where he is, don't come back until you've found him, or can be sure he has left the village. If you can't find him yourself, go and find a tracker to hunt him down. I'd rather he was brought here alive, but if he resists the commands of the Goddess, that is enough evidence for me - he must be guilty and may be killed. In that case, bring me his body."

He turned back to the woman in front of him. Amphissa. Widow of Macareus. To think that such a woman, the wife of such a man, should be assaulted, abused, abased by such as Carrin. Carrin! He couldn't so much as wield a practice sword, let alone fight with it! What was he thinking to enter a "trial of strength" with a woman. The one way in which they were inferior. Inferior, it would seem, even to a specimen as pathetic as Carrin. He struggled to control his temper.

"I am sorry that this has happened to you, Amphissa. I am so sorry for all the tragedies that have come to pass this year. This man is a nothing and I will deal with him like the flea that he is." Signalling to the other runner present in the lodge, he sent for the ritual swords.

A few fingers later, the first runner returned, followed by Carrin, looking downcast. The moment the runner had reached him, the enormity - and the stupidity - of what he had done had dawned on him. And the inevitable consequence.

"Look at me!" hissed Cronos. As he looked up, Cronos saw deep into his soul, and the range of possible futures resolved to just one. "I can see from your face that you know why you are here and that, in turn, tells me that you did what you are accused of and that you are guility. The Goddess has spoken and only has one judgment for assautling a woman of this community. You must fight her champion for supremacy. Only by winning can you indicate the Goddess' approval of your actions, and then you will be Cronos. Take up your sword."

Carrin looked at the two swords on the table dully. He was given the privilege of choice, but there was no value in that to him - how did he even know what to look for in a sword? He simply picked up the one that was closer to him and attempted to get into the ready position.

Before he had even raised the sword, Cronos had leapt forward, snatched up the other sword, bashed the sword out of his hand and, reversing his stroke, removed his head at the neck in a single, mighty blow. As his head went flying, the sword shattered. Cronos threw the haft down on the floor and knelt before Amphissa.

"My word, as the champion of the Goddess. He who did this has been judged and found wanting. He is no more. The law of the Goddess prevails, that is the privilege of hre priestesses to mate with whom they will, and not to be demeaned."

The others all shouted their assent. "Praise be the Goddess."
