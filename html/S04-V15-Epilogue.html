15. Epilogue

True to his word, Collatinus went and hunted down Sextus, then Tarquin Superbus himself.

In order to do that, he had to lift the siege of Ardea, but the troops there were happy enough about that; the siege had been boring for them too.

Once the Tarquins were dead, the Senate needed to decide on a new form of government. Should they just appoint a King without the legitimacy of a marriage to the Queen Carmenta? Or should they just appoint one interrex after another?

It was Brutus who provided the solution.

"No and no, but also yes and yes," he opined in the Senate. "It would offend the Gods for us to appoint a man on our own authority to be King, but it would also be wasteful to appoint a man every five days.

"Let us rather, then, choose two men, for the period of one year - the amount of time which, we are told, kings used to serve before Romulus decided to make himself rex perpetuus. We choose two men so that we do not have one tyrant over us, but rather two men who will balance each other out. In the same way that we understand that there were, at one time, two kings in a year, one to kill the other and to balance out the seasons.

"But we do not need to kill our representatives when they have served their year. They know, going in, that they are magistrates appointed by this body, in their prime of life. And that, once finished with their term, they can return to this body with their reputations enhanced.

"What say you?"

After some debate, the Senate decided to go with this plan. And the first two men elected as praetors for the city of Rome? Unsurprisingly, Brutus and Collatinus.

The plan largely worked well for four hundred years, until first Gaius Marius, then Lucius Cornelius Sulla, then Gaius Julius Caesar figured out how to circumvent the checks and balances in the system.
