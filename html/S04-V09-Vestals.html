9. Vestal Virgins

"And what stops their husbands fighting for the throne when I die?"

Numa looked at Romula: this was not an idle or rhetorical question. They now had five living daughters as well as their four (largely irrelevant) sons. While tradition held that the spirit of Carmenta travelled in the youngest daughter, that would not stop an enterprising husband of one of the older daughters from claiming otherwise (or even, if unscrupulous enough, eliminating the younger ones).

"You could always not marry them."

"How would I justify that? They are the most eligible maidens in the country. Men will come from near and far to marry them."

"And that is precisely your problem. Unscrupulous men. Men who will fight for power."

"So the question remains: how would I justify keeping our daughters virgins their entire lives?"

"You don't need to keep them virgins their entire lives: just long enough for Egeria to be married, and for her husband to solidify his claim to power once you have gone."

"Yes, yes, but how: tell me that, how?"

"Something religious, I would think. You have done enough there already. And so much of it has been suppressing the promiscuous sexual rituals that setting up your own daughters as virgins would be in line with that."

Numa pondered. He knew she was right. And she was certainly right about his suppression of the archaic female-dominated sexual orgies that had constituted the old religion. Well, in some ways, he'd had to. The only way in which he could be sure that his children inherited was to stop Romula from having sex with anyone other than him. And any number of other men felt the same way. From there it had been a small step to appointing a high priest - the Pontifex Maximus - when the old high priestess had died. Without a priestess to lead the orgies, they had died away, replaced by a bloodless religion based on animal sacrifice, rather than human.

"I am going to create a college of six virgins dedicated to Vesta, the Goddess of the Hearth."

He had called the Senate into session to promulgate his plan.

"As you know, Vesta is the Goddess of fire and flame, and is our only guarantor of fire in our hearths. But do we do enough to worship her? Last winter, in that dreadful storm, most of the fires throughout the city died. Only through the intervention of the Goddess herself did Jove agree to send a thunderbolt to set fire to that house in the forum. I now propose to build a temple to Vesta on that spot, and will dedicate four of my daughters to be her priestesses. They will be responsible for ensuring that there is always fire in one of the six hearths in the temple: one for each of the priestesses."

"Who will be the other two priestesses?"

"I am open to suggestions, Proculus. If you have a virgin daughter, you are welcome to put her name forward. As is anyone in this august body. There are only three conditions for consideration: firstly, the girl must be at least ten years old; secondly, she must be a virgin, unmarried and not even betrothed; and thirdly, she must be noble, and endowed with sufficient funds to provide for her needs for twenty years.

"For that is the time that we will expect our virgins to serve Vesta: twenty years. At the end of that time, they will be free to marry or not as they see fit."
