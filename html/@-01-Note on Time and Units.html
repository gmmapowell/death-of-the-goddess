Note on Time and Units

The ancients did not use our calendar, let alone our system of "epochs" - BC and AD. As far as I am aware, no such system existed in the timeframe of this book. More than that, in the first three sections of this book, all of the characters lived in a world with an infinite time horizon - a worldview in which each year was fundamentally a repetition of the previous year, and one in which the cycles of life were much more important than the passing of history.

Although the Romans later devised an epoch system AUC (from the founding of the City) which was dated to the presumed founding of Rome, it was not in use during the timeframe of this book.

At a smaller scale, I do not believe that the ancients of this book could count effectively, and have tried to use units which they would have been able to comprehend, both in time and space. Likewise, when counting people, I have assumed that their method would be to subdivide large groups into a manageable number (say, at most five or ten) of smaller groups, each of which would, again, be of a manageable size. In the first three sections of the book, you should not think of the entire population of the village being more than a thousand total individuals at any time.

For the benefit of modern readers, each of the four sections of this book (and some chapters, especifally in the fourth section) carries with it a tagline giving a modern day place and modern date. These are simply to help the reader imagine where and when the action is taking place and to put in within a familiar frame of reference. The characters in the book will not have had the same associations with these places and dates as the modern reader.

For those interested in more fully understanding the detailed assumptions I have made, there are appendices at the end of this book.
