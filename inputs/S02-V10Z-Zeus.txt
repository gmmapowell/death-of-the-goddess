@Chapter
title=Zeus


Cronos reviewed his battle dispositions.


The word had come to him at sunrise that an "unusual" army had been seen on the horizon.  Before the messenger had finished his message, he was up and girding himself for war.  He snapped off instructions to gather all the men of the village to prepare for battle and to meet the threat at the designated ready point.  Then he immediately hurried off to reconnoitre the situation.  He had not needed to check his instructions would be carried out: he had known they would be.


Now, all the men of the village where here, grouped by hands.  Their officers were in place.  The village was not particularly easy to defend, but this place offered him the opportunity to spread out his men and engage an enemy, who would usually be expected to have smaller numbers.  His usual tactic was to surround them - and obliterate them.  Of course, usually most of them ran away rather than stay to be obliterated.


Standing on a nearby rock, Cronos looked out at where the oncoming army lay, moving slowly towards them.  Was this really an army?  He understood the messenger's confusion now.  It was more like an entire village on the move.  Certainly, they mass contained some warriors: he could see swords and spears among them.  But there were women - not particularly unusual, but many of these were carrying babies with them - and animals mixed in amongst them.  Was this really an army?  Or was this a village on the move?  He had not heard news of any catastrophes that had destroyed nearby villages; but then, often the first news they would hear would come with those who were affected by it.  It was rare news that merited sending a special messenger between villages.


As he watched, the mass of humanity stopped and started to close in on themselves.  The warriors picked up flasks from women or from animals and drank - a good idea to keep up fluids before a long day of battle in the hot sun.  His men would do much the same in a moment or two before attacking.  Having drunk, the men started to group together and come towards the villagers again, readying swords and spears as they came.


About a furlong away, the men stopped and formed into rows.  One man, presumably the leader, stepped forward and harangued them:


"Ask-ken-hai Zeus, ah-ken!  Ask-ken-hai Zeus, ah-ken!"  he bellowed.  His men raised up a mighty shout and waved their weapons in the air.


"Final drink and then advance," yelled Cronos.  He moved into the lead, brandishing his sword.  Men moved around him, taking their places for the advance that would turn into an attack.  He had disciplined them well, but even so, no man looked forward to dying and he knew from experience that the forces would close slowly and timidly; only once the first blows had been struck would the two armies start a general engagement.  Cronos knew that his job was to be the one to make that happen; the one to lead the army and start the battle by engaging with whoever was at the forefront of the enemy forces.


The enemy leader continued to wave his spear and bawl unintelligibly.  Cronos had no idea who he was, but if his language was anything to go by, he was not from anywhere around here.  He started to advance, waving his sword at the man.


Suddenly the man charged at him, running at what must be close to full speed.  Insanity!  How could he manage a steady stance when running?  Surely he had a death wish.


When he closed within twenty paces, he reared back and _hurled_ his spear, straight into Cronos' chest.  Pierced through, Cronos fell to the ground, mortally wounded.  Around him, order turned into chaos as other men, mainly officers, were likewise impaled by the spears.  Here and there, men charged forwards, engaging the invaders, but to no avail.  Before the sun had even raised up a finger, the battle was over.  Those brave enough to engage were dead, and the rest either on their knees, begging for mercy or in full flight back to the village.


Zeus had arrived, and the village would never be the same.


--


The village was in uproar.


As soon as some of the men had come rushing back, telling of the death of Cronos and the enemy who threw spears and spoke gibberish they had never heard before, the panic had started. As men talked over each other, babbling and panicking, relating strange details of the behaviour, the word had spread, shouted from one end of the village to the other.  As more and more of the men had come back, confirming and expanding the story, and making it clear that the army truly had been routed, a deeper sense of doom had settled on the village.  Nobody quite knew what to do: should they all run away?  Where could they run to?  If the army could not stand up to these invaders, what could the remnants of the village do?


The situation was unprecedented.  From time to time in ages past, one or another Cronos had admitted defeat after a battle; the victorious enemy had then sat down and negotiated.  A certain number of goats, or a certain amount of wheat had been demanded and rendered; sometimes even some of the villagers had been handed over as slaves.  But never in the memory of or legend known by any of the villagers had Cronos been killed, or the army utterly routed.  It simply wasn't _possible_.


Except, of course, on this occasion it had happened.


Rhea entered the Megara and softly called for quiet.  Immediately the senior women in the village stopped talking and turned to face her.


"There is no doubt that a terrible tragedy has befallen us.  The Goddess has turned her face away from us; I do not know why.  But, nonetheless, we must stay calm.  These foreigners are here, and they are clearly stronger than us.  The Goddess clearly intends to use them to chastise us for our errors; so we must accept that.  If she chooses to use them to kill us, so be it;  but we must show Her that we trust in her wisdom.


"Now, gather together our people, and let us go to the edge of the village.  There we will kneel in reverence to the Goddess, showing Her that Her people show her respect when times are bad, even as we do when times are good.


"Now move."


--


Zeus called XXX over to him.


The boy had not lived up to his early promise knowing the languages of the south lands that they had passed through.  He had struggled to communicate with many of the peoples with whom they had come into contact.  Probably more people had died than strictly necessary because of that but, Zeus reflected, only two of those had been _his_ men.  If villagers wanted to die rather than submit, that was, at the end of the day, their choice.


He had also taken captives on his travels.  These _did_ know some of the local languages; but, sadly, Zeus could not communicate with them.  XXX could, however, and so now, as Zeus called him forward, he brought Lomaria with him.  He hadn't even bothered to negotiate with the men who had confronted him: they came armed and girded for a battle, so a battle he gave them.  But now that they had run away, he was prepared to negotiate.


As before, it seemed that while men around here were willing to fight, they wanted to leave the negotiation to the women.  Lomaria had said that was because in this part of the world women ruled, but that was obviously ridiculous.  How could women rule when they couldn't even stop a man having sex with them whenever he wanted to?  What kind of rule was it that wasn't based on strength and a willingness to fight, even to kill?


All of the village had turned out for him, it seemed.  All neatly arrayed, kneeling on the ground in front of him, heads bowed to the floor.  The kind of reverence he liked.  He straightened up the sword in his belt and issued his commands.


"XXX, take Lamaria and go and find out which of these women is in charge.  Tell her to come her and talk to me."


--


Rhea looked up at the three men who approached.


The big one on the right was clearly a warrior, and had to be the leader of this group.  Big and muscled, he had a bronze sword tucked in his belt, still bloody from the earlier battle.  The other two did not look like warriors.  Smaller, and somewhat diffident, they shuffled after the big man as if trying to hide in his shadow.


The big man opened his mouth and sounds came out.  Not words, but gibberish.  She understood what the returning warriors had meant now: this man was not from anywhere in the world that they knew, nor had the heard of it from any travellers who passed through these parts.  Diatribe finished, the second man started talking.  Some of his sounds seemed familiar, almost as if he were speaking but indistinctly.  Unlike the big man, he did not look at her, or anyone else in her party; he seemed to be talking just to the third man of their group.  Who started talking the moment the second man had finished.


"The Mighty Zeus says, 'I am Zeus.  By right of combat, I claim this village and all the people in it.  Now is the time I despise, the time of talking.  Tell me one reason why I should not stop this useless talking and just kill the lot of you.'"


The moment he started talking, Rhea flicked her eyes back to Zeus, trying to imagine him actually saying these words.  His original speech had been powerful and direct, full of confidence and assurance.  These two translators had none of his confidence.  But they had none of his strength.  She put the words together with the man and knew that he could do whatever he wanted: if he wanted to kill them all, he certainly could.


She stared at him while trying to choose the right words, the words that might stand a chance of being translated correctly.  Words that might keep some or all of her people alive.


"You have beaten us in battle.  The village is yours.  I recognise you as Cronos, and invite you to rule with me."


--


Zeus waited impatiently while the woman babbled in her 'language' and then while first Lomaria then XXX translated her words for him.


"And she says that you must now be something called Cronos - maybe she means King? - and says you can rule as long as you let her rule too."


"She says what?  Tell her of course I will rule, what possible reason could she have for thinking that I would share that with her?  She gives me what I already have if I want it."


Again, he waited, now playing with the handle of his sword while the long, long process of communicating with this woman took place.  He watched her like a hawk as she listened, then deliberated, then spoke.  He admired her lack of fear.  And yet, he felt, she would be better to be afraid.  She was unlikely to live as long as the Sun did today.


"She says that the ruler must be a woman in order to communicate with someone she calls the "goddess" - again, another title, I think - and that any man can only share in her rule."


Zeus looked at him in astonishment and rage.  So enraged, in fact, that he had started to draw his sword to kill him before he realized that the man was just a translator.  The woman in front of him was the originator of the comment.


"I care not about this goddess of hers.  Did he protect her village on this day?  I think not.  I spit on him and on her and on all of this village.  Tell her there is only one thing I need a woman for, but I do not need to give up my power in order to have her.  Tell her I will have her and then give her to all of my men to have after me if she doesn't do better than that."


He waited while the pair of them talked to each other, obviously struggling to figure out what to report to the woman.  Probably wondering if they should repeat his threats or just stick to facts.  The fact that the woman bowed her head and looked down at the ground again suggested that she had, at the very least, grasped the fact that she was being threatened.


"She says that she does not understand your contempt for her goddess but hopes that you will not suffer the fate of the condemned for your contempt.  She says that of course you can rape her, and of course you can kill her, but she asks what the benefit would be.


"And then, and then ... she asked if you would sacrifice her.  Take her life, kill her that the village may live.  You can settle here, and your men can marry the women here, and settle here.  And the village can continue to live.  It would be an offence to her 'goddess' if you were to destroy the village."


Zeus looked around.  As with other communities he had passed through, and as Lamaria had told him, the communities here had special magic that could make food appear out of the ground.  Lamaria himself did not know the rituals, but he did know that the deepest secrets of the rituals were all held by women.  It was the women of the village who were important.  And who could be cowed.


But who needed men?  Particularly weak men, who didn't know how to fight, and allowed _women_ to rule over them.  Zeus didn't.  Although he admired the courage of this one.


"Tell this woman she is now my junior wife.  Pass the word to kill all the men.  And any boys that you don't like the look of.  And then, tonight, we celebrate.  Make a grand bonfire and offering to the gods.  All the men can choose the women they like the look of, and claim them.  But no fighting!  There are plenty to go around."
