Clematis smiled at the weak joke.


The winter was generally a time of rest, but there were always jobs to be done in the fields.  Today, Clematis had oversight of a party of five men responsible for clearing out the channels and weeds around the dormant plants in one of the barley fields.  It was dull, back-breaking work in which the men basically worked on their hands and knees, grubbing through the moulting straw to find stray weeds and pull them out, while at the same time clearing out the irrigation ditches.  Elsewhere, other parties were responsible for carrying water from the river to the header tanks that fed the irrigation ditches.  Women, of course, carried the large pottery jars on their heads, but the men were generally responsible for the more laborious - if less skilled - task of filling them at the river and getting them up onto the women's heads.


The men, of course, were always lazy.  It was almost like a game with them - how close could they come to actually being punished before they gave way and did as they were told.  And Clematis, being still young had yet to develop the skills and talents of management that the older women put into practice on a daily basis.  She would learn, she had no doubt about that, but in the meantime she was struggling to control these men today and to get the work done at the speed that she - and she knew the women above her - expected.


Wayne had been assigned to her again.  In some ways, she liked his company, but he seemed to sense that and seemed to think that their "relationship" - a thing that really existed only in his mind - meant that she would let him slack off.  Uh, no.


The wicker basket they were filling with weeds was nearly full.  She felt that this should have happened some time ago - maybe a finger or two of the sun - and was impatiently trying to hurry them along.  Nothing she could say or do really cut through though.


"We could have a break," Wayne said.  "You and I could spend a few minutes together, then I reckon we would work harder after that.  Motivational, you might call it."


"We're behind as it is," she countered.  "If you want to have a break, you need to earn it."


"Basket's full, needs emptying," one of the others injected.  "Why don't you go dump it Wayne, you're young and could do with the exercise."


"Sure," said Wayne.  Then, to Clematis, "Do you want to come?  We could have some fun together, the two of us?"


She looked at him crookedly.  "No," she said.  "My job here is to supervise the actual work.  You run off and empty that basket now.  Good boy."


He stood up, picked the basket up and loped off to the edge of the field where all the detritus was dumped ready to be burnt at the end of the day.


"You don't get it, do you?" Jarn, one of the others, said to her.


"Get what?" she asked haughtily, feeling herself inadequate for not delivering the results she knew her mother would have managed.


"He likes you.  He just wants you to treat him nicely," Jarn said.  "And all you do is put him down like he's beneath your dignity."


Clematis looked at Jarn, genuinely confused.  Both of them were _men_, so yes, they were beneath her dignity.  She was a _woman_ now, and soon to be a _mother_.  What possible connection could she be expected to have with them?


Jarn huffed and looked away.  There was no use telling these young'uns.  Just because they had rank thrust on them, didn't mean they knew squat about how to get the best out of men doing these fundamentally unrewarding jobs.  Great.  Just threaten to beat them, or report them, or put them on the list for a sacrifice.  Highly motivational.  Keep on like that and most men here would be queuing up to die.  The only real thing worth living for were the half-moon celebrations, and half the time these stuck-up women didn't even pick you.  Even though there were more women than men.   Even if he didn't really share Wayne's frustration, he could understand it.  In a way this _girl_ couldn't.  She might be old enough to be counted a woman, but she wouldn't be a woman in his eyes until she started behaving like one.


Wayne came loping back with the basket.  The moment he put it down, the other men dumped the weeds they had been collecting in his absence in it.  He turned and smiled to Clematis.


"I did my best to be quick.  Didn't want you missing me," he chuckled.


Something inside Clematis froze, but thinking of what Jarn had just said to her, she decided not to go with the angry retort but to try and be encouraging.  To be, what had Jarn said, _nice_?


"Well, it's good to see you back," and she actually smiled at him.


"Thanks," he replied, smiled and got back to work.


Maybe there's something to this, she thought.

Later in the day, Wayne jogged back with another empty basket.  Everyone seemed to be happier, and the work was going more quickly.  Clematis seemed to think she was getting the hang of it.


"Glad to see me?" he asked, cheekily.

"Care to show me?" he challenged and, instead of getting back to work, took two steps towards her.


She froze.  Earlier in the day she would have slapped him down and they would have been in no doubt about their relative stations.  But as the afternoon had gone on, the atmosphere had become more convivial.  Suddenly, she didn't even feel in control.  What was going to happen here?
