The two men stood watching the sky.


Zeus and Helios stood side by side, with their polished bronze swords tucked into their waistbands.  The whole village had cleaned and tidied for the past week in readiness to welcome in the New Year.  Assuming it came.  Tonight, they were sure, would be the night, assuming that the Moon did not show her face tonight before the Sun showed his face above the horizon.  Slowly to the East, the sky brightened, obscuring the Seven Sisters - who had risen only a little while before - from view.


"O, Sun, we greet you!" chanted Helios as the disc slowly rose above the horizon.  "O, Sun, who brings us a New Year of hope!"


When they had finished greeting the Sun, this first day of the New Year, they walked back to the village.


"Today is the day of challenge," said Zeus.


"Do you think anyone will be foolish enough to challenge you?" asked Helios.  "I can't think that anyone has the strength to take you on in single combat."


"Perhaps not, but somebody has to challenge me, and I have to let them win," Zeus replied, a twinkle in his eyes.


"Or at least, not kill him," countered Helios. "That would be a waste."


"Not kill him _yet_," clarified Zeus.  "There will be time for my revenge later."


Helios clapped him on the back and they walked back to the village and Hera.


--


Hera stood next to Zeus in the Megara.


"This is Zeus, my husband.  By right of marriage, I submit to him and the powers of the Goddess which flow through me flow into him and he rules.  But, by the words of the Priest of the Sun, Helios, today marks the start of a New Year and is the day of challenge.  Any who do not accept Zeus as my husband may challenge him for the power.  If any overcome him, I will give myself to him and the power of the Goddess will flow from me to him, and he will be Zeus.


"All those who feel that Zeus is no longer the man to lead us should go home, arm and prepare to challenge Zeus in a duel to the death.  Only through death can the threads of power be diverted.  Challengers should present themselves here to fight Zeus when the Sun reaches her zenith."


Zeus stood stock still, impressive in his war gear with his brightly polished sword tucked in his belt and his bright, new tunic.  It would, indeed, take a brave man to think it worthwhile to challenge him, no matter what the rewards might be in the year ahead.


--


In the end, there were two.


As the villagers gathered around the edge of the Megara, the two men stood forward; like Zeus, they had clean, fresh tunics, brightly polished belts and swords.  By challenging Zeus for power, they guaranteed that either by the end of the day, they would be Zeus, dead, or banished.  The three men would never fight together again.


"Laumachos, step forward," challenged Zeus to the man to his left.  The better man, in Zeus' opinion, and one of his senior officers.  Yes, if any man of the village could defeat Zeus, it would be him.  And, maybe in a couple of years that might have come to pass.  Zeus counted himself fortunate that the man had chosen this year to make his challenge, before Zeus inevitably started to decline.  Then, of course, it was only right that a younger, stronger man challenge him as leader: such was the way of the world.


Laumachos moved into the centre of the Megara as the other man retreated into the crowd for now.  As second challenger, he knew he had an advantage: Zeus would be worn down by the first bout and would have less strength to fight off a second challenger.


Laumachos drew his sword in a fluid motion, keeping both eyes on Zeus and started to circle.  They had often sparred in training and he knew the measure of the man he challenged: his better, it was true, but he himself was getting older and in years to come younger men would be able to challenge either Zeus or himself - whoever was leader.  It was this year or never.  Probably never, he acknowledged.


Suddenly Zeus drew his sword and charged at him, throwing down a heavy overhanded blow in a single motion that flowed from the draw.  Laumachos had his sword up and in perfect form for the parry.  The clanging of the bronze swords echoed deafeningly around the Megara.  Laumachos pushed up and backed away.  Zeus pulled his sword back and swung a wicked backhanded swipe at Laumachos' head.  Laumachos again parried in perfect form, then spun around behind Zeus, who followed the spin, engaging swords.  A step to the left, and Laumachos disengaged.  The watching crowd drew in a collective gasp as Zeus swung again.  Three blows, all perfectly parried.


Laumachos jumped backwards, then turned defence into attack.  A quick combination of blows had Zeus on the defensive.  But then Zeus stepped to his right, slammed his sword down on Laumachos', and then used his shoulder to bash the challenger to the floor.  Stepping forward, he placed his foot on Laumachos' sword and then pointed his own at the man's throat.  Laumachos dropped his sword and said the ritual phrase, "I acknowledge the power of Zeus."


Zeus stepped back and climbed onto the dais with Hera.  Straightening his tunic, he tucked his sword into his belt while Laumachos stood up and retreated into the crowd of villagers.  Meanwhile, the Marshal came forward and collected his sword.  As a failed challenger, Laumachos could no longer have a sword, but it was too valuable to just leave lying around.  The Marshal would make sure that it was properly accounted for.


Hera looked at the other challenger: "Oh, Kamiades, do you still wish to challenge the Mighty Zeus, my husband, to be ruler over the village?"


Kamiades stepped forward from the crowd, his bronze sword shining as he drew it.  "Indeed I do, Queen Hera.  I challenge Zeus for your hand and to lead this great people in the worship of the Goddess."


Zeus stepped back down from the dais with his hand on his sword and approached Kamiades.  Without waiting for Zeus to draw, Kamiades stepped forward and snapped off an overhead blow.  Faster than the eye could track, Zeus had drawn his sword and parried the blow.  Stepping inside the blow to his left, he counter-attacked with a series of chops, left and right, pulling many of them before they had even been parried.  Quickly, what should be a duel became almost a dance as the two men offered blows only to pull them, each trying to induce the other into a mistake.


Kamiades drew first blood, drawing a thin line across Zeus' left wrist.  As the crowd sucked in a collective breath, Zeus took advantage of his opponent's surprise to launch a furious attack in combination, ultimately breaking the challenger's sword.  He drew back for what would have been a slash to take the man's head off, but, staring into the man's eyes, held the blow.  For several heartbeats, the two of them stared into each other's eyes.


Then Kamiades dropped to his knees and released the hilt of his sword.  Zeus lowered his sword until it rested on the back of the man's head.


"I acknowledge the power of Zeus," Kamiades conceded.  Zeus removed his sword and tucked it back into his belt.  Stretching out his hand, he raised Kamiades to his feet.


"You fought well, old friend, but you know the law.  Those who challenge Zeus must either succeed or fail.  To fail is to be banished.  You will have your one cow, a goat, any of your own possessions, and any villagers who choose may go with you.  Your life will not be easy as a scapegoat, but for the history we have together, I wish you well."


Kamiades bowed his head, "I thank my Lord Zeus.  I did not challenge you because I doubted you, but because the Goddess wished it.  I am pleased that she found no fault in you, O Lord.'  Thus saying, he turned and left.


Zeus called Laumachos forward.  "Well done, old friend.  Of the challengers, none could beat me, but you challenged well and fought well and shall have your reward."


Turning to the villagers he raised his hands above his head.  "Now, O villagers, attend, for in acknowledgment of my friend Laumachos' achievement today, I pronounce him King and Zeus."  Snapping his fingers to an attendant, the man brought forward a tray.  From it, he removed a circlet of laurel leaves.  "With this crown, I anoint you, Laumachos."  He reached and removed two goblets from the tray.  Passing one to Laumachos, he said, "An offering from Dionysus to bless your union with Hera.  Here, drink with me to share our good fortune."


Linking arms, they drank the goblets dry and then threw the clay vessels across the Megara to smash against the wall.  Drawing him forward, Zeus presented Laumachos to Hera.


"Hera, this man is now your husband.  This I command, that you take him and marry him and celebrate the New Year with him."


Laumachos let go of Zeus and walked over to Hera.  Taking her in his arms, he kissed her, then raised her shift over her head.  She, in turn, removed his belt and tunic.  Then sinking down onto the floor of the dais, she pulled him on top of her, then, stroking his penis until it became fully erect, she pulled him inside her, then laid back and let herself go to the ecstasy of this man who was now her husband as the villagers looked on, calling encouragement to the man who had entered the room as Laumachos, but now, having entered Hera, was called Zeus.


The man who had entered the room called Zeus watched for a while as the new Zeus used his wife, bringing her to orgastic pleasure, then turned and left the village, denied his title, his name, his kingdom.


--


The fire burned at dusk to welcome the New Year as it always had.


The women of the village who had dedicated their lives to the Goddess danced around the fire, as they always had.  As ever, they were led in their worship by Athena, dedicated priestess of the Goddess.  And, as ever, they were joined by Hera, through whom the Goddess presented herself to the world.


The drummers played the beat, joined by the flute players and accompanied on the lyre by Apollo.  The dancers performed the well-rehearsed steps of the ritual in time with the beat, steadily increasing in tempo.


From time to time, men would stand up from their places surrounding the fire and, finding their way into the rhythm of the music, would join the dance, forming an outer circle, facing in towards the women and the fire.  Dancing in opposite directions around the fire, each of the men would pass all of the women, and, more importantly, each of the men would be on display before the women for when the time of selection came.


From his place in the front row, the man who had been Zeus since his challenge at noon rose up and joined the dance, performing the ritual steps in time to the music and in perfect alignment with all those around him.  Seamlessly, he entered the circle of men and rotated with them, looking at each of the women he passed and smiling at them.  In his former life as an army hero, he had had most of these women, knew them intimitately and had worshipped the Goddess with them.  But tonight, they knew he was not the man for them, however much he smiled at them, no matter how many times he passed them.  No, as Zeus, he was reserved for Hera, his wife.


The fire crackled and spat gouts of flame.  Its roar competed with the noise of the drums and the music, but even so all the dancers heard it when the music suddenly changed: the tempo dropped and the drums became less intense.  This was now the time of the selection, although now the ritual dictated that Hera could only make one selection: Zeus.


The circles stopped with Hera and Zeus facing each other.  The music stopped too.  Hera pointed at Zeus and called above the noise of the flames:


"Zeus, I call you.  Come to me to demonstrate the fertility of the land.  Let us show the grain how to rise.  Let it be Spring again, then Summer!  Let us cast off the garments of death, and celebrate the act of life!"  And with that, she reached out her hands in offering.


Zeus stepped forward, taking her hands in his, then pulling her closer.  As they acted the ritual of magic, he came closer to her and lifted her high against his body.  The music started again, and to its rhythm their bodies swirled around her as, for the second time that day, he entered her and showed the Goddess that they understood the act of procreation, and through their efforts would encourage the grain to rise.


Around them, the other couples in the dance followed suit, all determined to ensure their acts led to a healthy grain crop this year,  without which the village might starve.  Slowly the bodies sank to the ground in various positions, pumping away, celebrating the life that was returning after the death of winter.


Zeus lifted himself up off Hera as he finished, flushed and pleased with himself.  "Oh, it is great to be Zeus," he declared.


Suddenly a man bounded out from the edge of the circle, barely seen in the flickering firelight.  The light shone and dazzled from something in his hand - a bronze sword.  Leaping forward, he swung the sword through Zeus' neck, severing it in a single blow a causing a gush of blood to fountain from his arteries, spreading over Hera, the ground, and other dancers nearby.


The man picked up the head by its hair and turned to face the villagers.  "The old year is dead," he declaimed.  "Zeus is dead.  The old Zeus is dead."  And then he lifted the head up high.  The man who had been Zeus, then not been Zeus, was Zeus again.  Carelessly, he tossed the head of his rival into the fire.


"Long live Zeus! Long live Zeus!" chanted the villagers.


Behind living Zeus, his wife hacked the body of the dead Zeus to pieces, again in accordance with time-honoured ritual.  And threw most of him into the fire.  The little she kept - shrunk after its earlier exertion - was dedicated to the Goddess.  As it ever had been.
