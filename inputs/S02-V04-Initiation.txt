title=Initiation


Metapontus and Arne were admitted to the Megara.


Cronos and Rhea were seated, dealing with the everyday administrative business of the village.  When they had finished dealing with the previous item, Metapontus and Arne approached and bowed low.


"My daughter has come of age," declared Metapontus, not entirely without pride, for today his daughter had taken the first step towards being a full member of the community.


"And which path do you wish her to take?" enquired Cronos.  "Do you have a husband picked out for her, or do you wish her to be a priestess?"


The single biggest decision in a woman's life: to be attached to one man, and to have the simplicity, consistency and, some said, security of a nuclear family; or, by rejecting that, to be free to serve the Goddess completely; and, in time, to have the possibility of real rank in the community and to have her children grow up among the servants of the Goddess.


For each girl becoming a woman, it was an individual choice, but a choice so important that only her parents, with the maturity and wisdom that comes from a long life, could hope to make it correctly.  And, even then, they were well advised to consult with others, especially the leaders in the community.


"There is a young man we think might be a good choice," said Metapontus, "who is not unknown to you.  As yet he does not have a wife, but we think he would like one.  And our Amphissa might be a good choice."


One of the issues, of course, was simply numeric: the attrition rate among males, particularly younger males once they had reached maturity, was significantly higher than it was for females, even taking into account the risk of death in childbirth.  Once a man was married, he and his wife formed a family.  While he could still partake in ritual intercourse at the frequent feasts, he could not marry again.  So while for many, the life of a priestess was a choice, for others it simply came down to: is there a man available to marry?  And one that will live for long enough to make the marriage worthwhile?  And one that is worth marrying?  For, once chosen, the path to marriage was irrevocable: both man and woman were committed for the rest of their lives, regardless what happened to their partner.


"Macareus," Cronos guessed.  Metapontus nodded.  In some senses, it wasn't even a guess: Macareus was a promising and handy young man, not married, and reaching that age where he had demonstrated sufficient skill with a blade that he was more, rather than less, likely to survive in any combat.


"Have you considered waiting?" Rhea interjected.  Because, while it was a binary choice, it was also possible to wait:  a woman could wait many years before deciding that a husband was the right choice for her, living the life of a priestess and having children along the way.  On the upside, that proved her fertility in the eyes of the Goddess; on the other hand, many men were jealous and did not want a wife who came with three or four children who were not his.  What was the point of marriage, anyway, if not that a man knew that the children in his house were _his_ children, formed from his sexual union with _his_ wife.


"Yes, but not seriously," responded Metapontus.  "Unlike our other daughters, she is very much that sort of girl.  She likes the simple life and does not aspire to communion with the Goddess.  And Macareus is a good man, who we are sure will look after her.  He often shares his meals with us and sits with us at the festivals.  I am sure there is attraction there, and I am sure that it is mutual.  Obviously, she must wait the ritual period, but after that, if he confirms he will have her, I would have her married to Macareus."


"I feel the same way, " said Arne.  "She is so like me in so many ways.  Rhea, you may remember that I decided to wait and see how the life of a priestess felt, but it was not one for me, even though I grew up in that environment.  I wanted the security of a good man to wrap my arms around at night rather than the ecstasy of the Goddess.  And I have never looked back on that decision."


The benefit of waiting, of course, for the daughter, was that, by becoming a priestess even temporarily, she gained autonomy and the ability to choose her own husband - should she wish to do so.


"Very good," said Rhea.  "Let Macareus be summoned."


--


Macareus put down the heavy rocks he had been lifting.


"Cronos wants me in the Megara?  Not on the drill field?" he clarified with the messenger.


"Yes, sir," the boy confirmed.  "I was sent to summon you to audience with the King and Queen in the Megara.  You are to go there immediately."  He didn't need to say it was important: a summons to appear in the Megara was always important.


Cronos walked over to the cupboard by his bunk in the barracks and pulled out his "quality" garment.  Slipping it on over his head and bare chest he indicated to the boy that he would follow him.


His mind was racing.  Had he failed in some task and was to be punished?  Or was he to be given a promotion to a leader of a hand?  Or was there some special mission at hand?  They couldn't be under attack, he was sure.  That summons would not be to the Megara, but to the battle site.  And would have been sounded on the trumpet.  He hoped he hadn't failed in anything, but his conscience was clear.  He didn't _think_ he had done anything wrong.


He followed the boy up the two steps and into the Megara, where he found Cronos and Rhea giving audience to a friend of his - Metapontus - and his wife, Arne.  He almost greeted them before remembering his station and who had summoned him.  He bowed low to Cronos and Rhea.


"Your majesties," he intoned.  "How may I serve the village?"


"This is not a question of service," replied Cronos, "but of your future."


"Metapontus and Arne have a daughter, Amphissa," said Rhea, "whom I believe you already know.  She has today demonstrated through the shedding of blood that she is ready to take her place as a full member of our society.  Her parents think that she would be better served through marriage than as a priestess, and their choice of husband falls on you.  What do you say?"


Macareus was gobsmacked.  Of all the things he had considered since the boy had summoned him to this meeting, this one had never crossed his mind.  Marriage?  Responsibility?  Living with just one woman rather than the common life of a soldier in the barracks?  He struggled to see any of the upsides.  Most men did marry, though, having a desire to leave children behind them.  In some sense the barracks was a revolving door of young men arriving, growing up, and then moving out to start to marry and start families.  He had just never thought about taking that step for himself.


Not that he knew how many children he had, of course, among the priestesses.  He performed his duties diligently - and enjoyably - whenever he was chosen in the rituals by a priestess.  But that was just a few minutes' service to the Goddess.  He had seen Metapontus over the years, tied down by his family commitments and responsibility to his children - a much tighter responsibility than Macareus felt for the priestesses' children held in common by the village.


Cronos saw the emotions playing across the face of his underling - and friend.  "You do not need to decide today, my friend.  For today she belongs to the Goddess, then to me, and then she will serve with the priestesses until the moon sets on her ritual period.  When the moon sets that night, you can claim her, or she can become a priestess."


--


When Amphissa returned from "the women's hut" out in the fallow field, she was brought to Cronos in the hut he shared with Rhea.  Rhea was tactfully and understandably absent.  Although Amphissa was aware of the mechanics of what was going to happen from attending the rituals, this would obviously be her first time.


Cronos started by trying to engage her in general conversation, although the girl seemed very shy and withdrawn.  He tried a different tack.


"I believe you know that your parents would like you to marry to one of my full-time soldiers, Macareus.  Is that what you think you would like, the life of a married woman; or would you prefer to be a priestess to the Goddess?"


She looked at him, surprised.  Was the great leader of the community really interested in the affairs of a couple of young people of no consequence in the community?  What answer did he want?


"I don't know," she stammered, hesitantly.  "If that is what they think is best for me, I'm sure they're right.  And yes, I like Macareus.  He comes to our hut quite often and seems very nice."


Cronos nodded.  Nothing this girl had said or done so far had impressed him; probably life as the wife of a soldier would be the best for her.  She didn't seem to have what it took for the much more competitive life of a priestess with the associated responsibilities.


He decided to cut to the chase: she didn't seem to be relaxing at all; quite the opposite.  "You know why you're here," he confirmed with her.  She nodded.  "Shall we just get on with it?" he asked.


"You're the King," she replied.  "Whatever you say."


So it was going to be like that, was it?  You're the King; I'll do what you say; impress me with your abilities and technique.  So be it.


"Well, come and lie down here," he soothed, "and take those garments off."  As he said that, he removed his own loincloth and threw it to one side.  Then he lay down beside her, and used his hands to soothe her.  She relaxed a little, then tensed again.  He gave up on all the subtle approaches, climbed on top of her, used his right hand to prepare the way and pushed inside her.  She uttered a little "ow" of pain as he entered her and the blood spilt on the straw of the bedding, then flopped back to let him do what he wanted with her.


It was over quickly.


--


Over the course of the next few days, she came to his hut a number of times, each time building her understanding and confidence.  When the festival of the last quarter of the moon came around, she was ready and took her part with the priestesses and did an acceptable job.  Cronos was surprised that she did not choose Macareus to be her partner.  Did that mean that she didn't want to marry him?  Or that, if she did want to marry him, she wanted to experience someone else while she was still able to?


The month wore down and as they prepared for the festival of the turning of the month, Macareus came to see Cronos and Rhea formally at the time of meeting and petitions.  After being introduced and bowing low to the couple, he declared his intentions.


"If it is still possible, I would like to take the young woman Amphissa to be my wife."


"Very good, very good," enthused Cronos.  "And I am sure she will make you a good wife.  You know that it is the festival of the new Moon tonight; she will be with the priestesses.  When she calls you, she will call you to be her husband."


"Indeed," said Rhea. "I will talk to her and make sure she is aware of the ritual."


--


The fire was burning brightly, and the priestesses were dancing.  In the audience, instruments were being played.  As ever, Rhea was leading the dance and called Cronos to serve her.  When they had finished, Rhea rose up again and pointed to Amphissa, strategically placed to her right for this very moment.  As she did so, Amphissa left the circle of dancing women and went out into the crowd, pulling Macareus back with her.  But, instead of just initiating sex with him, she knelt on the earth at his feet.


The music stopped.


Apart from the noise of the burning fire, you could have heard a bracelet drop.  The silence and stillness was almost palpable.
