@Chapter
title=Zeus


Zeus lunged, his intent to kill.


What had started as a drunken argument had rapidly escalated into a battle to the death.  The rest of the company, who had been watching and laughing as the argument spiralled hilariously out of control were now rapt and alert as they recognised the death struggle for what it was.


Cadmus blocked the lunge and threw back a riposte.  He knew he was outmatched in this duel and, had he been sober enough, would have found a way out of it.  Not that he would have been able to avoid it forever.  Zeus believed - incorrectly, but not without reason - that he had had sex with Hera, and Zeus was very jealous of his wife; even if he himself was anything but faithful.


Zeus blocked the thrust and hacked down towards Cadmus' head.  Cadmus ducked and slid to the side, moving out of Zeus' swing and then turning and batting the sword away.  He took two steps back and drew a few quick breaths.  "We could ..." he began, but Zeus charged at him and swung again.  Ducking, he turned away.  There was no getting out of this now, so he needed to fully commit to the fight.  Rapidly sobering up, he realised that even sober, he would probably lose to a drunk Zeus.  Zeus was big, and powerful, and fast.  And, he had to admit, spent a lot longer practising with his sword than Cadmus was ever prepared to do.


He quickly gasped a couple of breaths, then leapt to his left, swinging his sword as he did so.  Zeus took it on his blade and then twisted inside his guard.  With his left elbow, he smacked Cadmus across the face and jumped back.


Stunned, Cadmus took a moment to clear his head.  A moment that could have been fatal, except, he now realised, Zeus was playing with him, like a cat with a mouse.  His head ringing, he raised his sword and faced Zeus again.  Zeus lifted his sword above his head and swung down.  Just in time, Cadmus put his blade up and blocked the blow.  But Zeus was strong: Cadmus' blade first shivered, then snapped. He was left with just half a cubit of blade in his hand.

Zeus laughed, long and loud.  "Are you ready to die, Cadmus?" he asked.  With the flat of his blade, he smacked Cadmus across the head, knocking him to the ground.  What was left of Cadmus' sword fell from his hand moments before Zeus swung his sword through his arm, chopping it in half.  Cadmus screamed in pain.


Zeus came and stood over him, still laughing at the sight, waiting for the scream to end.  Cadmus looked up at him, eyes wide and showing the whites with pain.  He felt he had one more thing to do in this life, in the handful of heartbeats before his inevitable death.  "I swear, Hera was never unfaithful to you with me."


Zeus plunged his sword into Cadmus' neck, sending a fountain of blood up into the air.


"That was fun," he declared.  "Anyone else fancy a fight?"


Nobody would even meet his gaze.


--


Zeus led the ox up the hill.


Augur had prepared the altar and chopped the wood for the fire.  He had also prepared the stick with which he would start the fire.  It was sharpened at one end and placed in a plank with a hole cut from it, and covered in small chippings of wood called tinder.  It was a lot of effort to light a fire this way, but in the absence of lava provided by a volcano - or a chance fire in a forest - it was the only reliable way to start a fire from scratch, which is what was demanded for this ritual.


Celeus stepped forward as Zeus came near the altar, and with a single blow, struck the ox on the forehead with a mallet, stunning it and causing it to slump to the ground.  The three men picked the ox up and placed it on the altar.  Augur drew his knife and slit its throat, catching the blood in a bowl in his other hand.


Zeus moved forward to where the fire stick was and started rotating it back and forth between his hands until a spark lit the tinder Augur had arranged around it.  As the tinder caught, Zeus removed the fire stick from the hole and moved the block onto the chopped wood for the fire.  Slowly it started to smoulder and then burn.  As it did so, Celeus came and poured the bowl of blood onto the wood - the first offering to the Gods of the Sky.


Meanwhile Augur took his knife and started to slit the belly of the beast.  Quickly, he extracted the liver and the heart and put them on a platter.  The rest of the entrails of the beast he let slither out and carried them to the burgeoning fire.  Where the fat landed, it immediately sizzled and burnt, sticking to the pile of wood.  The rest of the slippery mess fell about the stack of wood, flashing and gleaming in the burning light.


Augur used his knife to slice up the liver and examined in closely.  Then he did the same with the heart.  When he had finished, he took them over to the fire and, using his knife to keep his hand out of the fire, carefully placed them on the burning pile.


Zeus took an axe and cut off the ox's head.  This he added to the steadily growing pile of ox parts on the pile of firewood.  The flames were now licking greedily around the various parts of flesh, blackening and consuming them.


Once the Gods were appeased, four men stepped forward, bringing the spit with them.  They set up the stands to the spit either side of the fire pile and then skewered the ox through with the spit.  They carried that over to the fire and placed it on the stands.  In short order, the ox was starting to cook and the air was filled with the smell of burning and roasting flesh.  Somewhere, somebody had broached a cask of beer and started pouring it into cups and handing them around.  Before long, all the men of the tribe were happily merry and singing songs while waiting for the meat to roast.


Zeus walked up to Augur.  "What news?" he asked.  "What do the Gods say we should do?"


Augur shook his head confused.  Others always seemed to think that the Gods communicated in the same way that people did; but they didn't.  Messages from the Gods were not just cryptic, they were sent in ways that most men could not even understand.  The signs of the heart and liver had been unusual, but he felt confident in his interpretation nevertheless.


"They say that we must be obedient," he replied.  "And we must follow the sign," he added, a confused look crossing his face.


"The sign?" asked Zeus.  "What sign?"


"I don't know," replied Augur.  "Except that I feel in my bones we will be shown it soon, possibly tonight.  And I feel sure that the sign will be unequivocal in its message.  Wait up with me this night; I know you long for your tent and the warmth of Hera's body, but trust me: this night something will happen and we should be ready for it."


Zeus nodded, externally calm but internally seething.  Why did everyone seem to think that he was under Hera's thumb?  Yes, she was an exciting, attractive woman, and the mother of his children, but she was just a pastime: she never came between him and his duty.


Slowly, the ox roasted and before long Celeus and the others were slicing meat from the carcass with their swords and handing it out to the men gathered around the fire.  Zeus and Augur were off to one side, watching the sky.  Celeus brought them some meat and sat down with them.


"Are you searching for a sign?" he asked.  "Do you know what or where?"


"No," replied Augur.  "I only know that we must be attentive for a sign."


The three of them sat, watching the sky, trying to cover all directions when suddenly the ground starting to move beneath them.  All three of them jumped up, awestruck.  This was definitely a sign.  A sign that the Gods walked among them.  A sign that many men lived many years and never had.  Having leapt to their feet in shock and surprise, all three now knelt down, prostrating themselves before the power that none of them could resist or imagine.


On the horizon, they saw a burst of flame appear.  A volcano! One of the Gods was walking among them and demanded their worship on that far hill.  Zeus looked at the other two men with him and knew that all three of them were thinking the same thing: this was not just a sign, this was a clear sign that the Gods were among them and demanding their audience.  To the south.  In the morning, they would set off, travelling south.


The ground shook again, violently.  From the hill to their left, rocks came loose and rolled down towards them.  In the morning the would go south.


If they lived that long.


--


Zeus and Augur climbed up the side of the volcano to where they could walk past the lava flow.  Here was where the Gods would speak to them, if they were reverent and brave.  They had left the rest of the tribe down below, fearful and fretting over the awesome power of their Gods, of which this was just the most recent manifestation.  If they failed to return after two days, the tribe would choose a new leader and a new plan: either to follow in their footsteps or to acknowledge that the Gods had not wanted mere mortals to trespass on the sacred mountain.


The ground continued to rumble under their feet.  No matter how many times he saw the flows of lava down mountainsides, Zeus continued to be in awe.  The power of a river of fire; the heat thrown off; the destruction they could cause - simply melting rocks that he would have spent hours trying to smash with a hammer; and then the way that at the end the lava just gave up and _became_ a rock - by some means that was completely opaque to him.  It was all simply fascinating.  He could have stayed here forever, absorbed in the mysteries of the Gods.


Except it wasn't healthy.


Everyone knew that the Gods did not welcome humans trespassing on their territory.  Apart from the obvious death that came from the lava flows, many had died from some kind of ecstasy after having been exposed to the Gods for too long, struggled to breathe and died.


He was here on a mission and he knew it.  For too long now, his tribe had struggled to find a space to call their own in these lands.  Other tribes had come, and all the tribes had grown too numerous.  Every year, it was harder to find water away from the major rivers, and the grasslands were increasingly being eaten away by the flocks and herds.  The pressure of the nomadic lifestyle was causing an ever-increasing number of battles and wars between tribes competing for the same scarce resources.  But what were they to do?


Zeus and Augur stood, watching the lava flow down the mountain, melting the rocks that were obstacles to its flow.  Zeus understood that in some way the message was for him to do the same thing: remove the obstacles in his way.  But he could not see how.


Suddenly, slightly up the hill and to their left, a patch of lava exploded upwards in a ball of steam.  As it did so, a new tributary opened up across the mountainside, with a rush of lava down the mountainside.  As it plunged down the hill, the red river suddenly hit a pool of water and exploded in an eruption of steam.  Zeus stared, once again awestruck by the sight.  To be here was truly to be in the presence of the God.  He pulled his eyes away, forced himself to look at Augur, in order to check that the man alongside him, whose job it was to interpret the signs of the Gods, had seen the same thing that he, the leader of the tribe, had seen.  And what he saw in Augur's face stunned him.  It was a mixture of shock, reverence and ... _understanding_.


"Truly, Indra has spoken to us tonight," said Augur.  "For all the times that I have said that the Gods speak to us with forked tongues and refuse to make their message plain, today is the exception.  There can be no doubt that Indra has come down from his mountain and shown us what we must do."


"What?" asked Zeus, his eyes gleaming from excitement and reflecting the blazing light of the lava.  "What do the Gods tell us we must do."

"We are trapped in this valley," explained Augur.  "We cannot find space to live because of the others around us upon whom we war.  But this valley is not the whole world.  If we climb up," he pointed up and around to the mountains that surrounded them, "then we can find a way through.  We can go that way," now pointing the way the lava had split off, roughly south although a little west.  "And Indra will show us the way.  There will be mountains, there will be rivers, there will be seas.  And there will be a land flowing in milk and riches.  When we arrive in that land, we will be masters of our destiny.


"The Gods have shown us that not all of us will make it.  The journey will be long and hard, and there will be many obstacles.  But for those who arrive, it will be a land of plenty.  I may not make it; many may not make it, but the Gods say, and I cannot doubt them, that you, Zeus, will make it and will be long remembered in that land as the very agent of the Gods, coming down out of the mountain to take the world."


Zeus blinked.  He could hardly believe it.
