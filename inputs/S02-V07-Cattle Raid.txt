title=Cattle Raid


"I'm concerned that our cattle stocks are getting low," Cronos said.


Rhea looked at him intently.  "It's true that we have lost more this year than we would have liked," she conceded.


"And they have not bred as well as could have been expected.  I don't know why, but I suspect that some of the bulls are not getting the job done."


"Unlike you," she grinned, patting her stomach where her pregnancy was now clearly showing.


Cronos smiled back at her: "It is a shame I will not live to see her born."


"It will not be the first time I will celebrate the New Year ritual carrying Cronos' child."


Cronos brought her back to the topic at hand; the one which had been occupying his mind now for the last half moon cycle.  "If the bulls do not quicken the cows, and we do not increase the size of our herds, some will starve before the next harvest."


"The Goddess will provide, Cronos, she always does."


Cronos stared at her.  While her faith in the Goddess was absolute, and could not be questioned, he knew the facts to be different.  The Goddess did _not_ always provide; in some years the food ran low - or even out - and in the absence of cattle to kill, villagers would starve.  The harvest this year had been good, but not great.  Now was the time to act, he knew it.  Another couple of months and the breeding time would be past.  Then it would be too late to act.  A dozen head of cattle - two or three bulls and the rest cows - might make all the difference to how many lived or died this winter.


"We need more cattle," he asserted.  "Let me convince you," pulling her close to him.


--


The moon was nearly full.  Ideal for a cattle raid like this.


Cronos had led his armed men to within a mile or two of the target village during the afternoon, then, as the sun was setting, he had led them into this gulch, hiding from the sentries and patrols he assumed would be protecting the village.  Not to do so would be foolish. Overhead, the moon was rising to the east, almost a hand up in the sky now.  To the west, the sky was still bright with the fading sunset.


He looked around him at the men crouching in the gully.  They were all ready for action, although hopefully there would not be any action until the moon was at its highest.  Men, even the best and most alert, were never as alert in the depths of the night; they would be convinced that they were wasting their time, and that nobody would raid here, now, in the middle of the night.  Their thoughts would wonder, either to the warm, comfortable bed that they could be sharing with a loved one, or else to thoughts of what tomorrow might bring.  His men had no such distractions: they knew that the raid would be launched in a few hours when they were ready and the conditions were favourable.


Of course, they still needed to be prepared for any surprises.  The village might have patrols out, any of which might come across them hiding here.  If that happened, their mission would be foiled - the element of surprise would have gone - but they would still be required to fight, but in order to defend themselves, not for the more noble purpose of stealing cattle.


Assuming all went well, they would have to make off with the cattle, taking a roundabout route to hide their true destination - after all, it would do them no good if these villagers came tomorrow night to take their cattle back - and that was best done under the light of the failing moon, say, giving themselves a quarter of the night to get away before sunrise and the village would mount an effective pursuit.  By then, they wanted to be far gone.  Other than that, though, the later the attack came the more likely they were to find guards tired and bored with nighttime duty.


--


After it had gone fully dark, Cronos had led a hand of men forward into an advance position where they could see the guards and the cattle pen.  The pen was outside the village proper, but was still guarded by the villagers.  Cronos could see two guards inside the pen, and figured that there would be a couple more in the village near the pen.  Ideally, he would have already had a couple of men dismantling the fence, preparing the way for when they were trying to steal the cattle, but that wouldn't be possible under the noses of the guards.  On top of which, killing or disabling the guards would be almost impossible without alerting the guards in the village.  Unless they both happened to nod off for a while, this was going to be tricky.


Cronos stole back to the gulch to brief the others.


Suddenly, a racket emerged from the darkness in front of him.  As he lurched forward, he could see the outline of a couple of men he did not recognise, thrusting spears into the gulch.  Around them, his own men boiled up to engage them in combat.  Of all the bad luck!  A village patrol had happened upon them by what must be absolute chance.  Darting forward, he pulled out his sword and swung it across the back of the legs of one of the men, cutting his tendons and felling him instantly.  Immediately after, he saw the other pulled down into the gulch by two of his men and disarmed.  Quickly, both men were killed, fortuitously quietly.


But the fight had not gone unnoticed.


"Ho! Patrol!" came the shout from somewhere in the direction of the village.  There was no answer, of course: both men in the patrol were dead.  But unquestionably, the guards would be alert now, and know that somebody - or possibly something - was out here threatening their village.  The raid could not happen now; or at least, could not happen with the element of surprise that was so key to success.  In a sense, the roles were reversed, since, outnumbered as they were, they would not be able to fight a defensive action if the village mustered all their forces and sent them out here.


What to do?  What to do?  Although he had been on raids before, he had never been Cronos before - never had the responsibility to _decide_.  The plan had been a good one.


Turning around, he bent down in a crouch and hurried back to the forward position.  His men spotted him as soon as he spotted them - they had heard both the commotion and the challenge, but had remained where they were until he came to give them further instructions.  In a few heartbeats, he was on his knees next to them, indicating with his hands that the only option he saw was retreat.  And they should come with him.


Stealing off into the quiet night, they left two dead guards behind them.  And a dozen prize cattle that might be the difference between life and death this winter.
